# Saint Christopher and Nevis Citizenship (Marriage and Child Applications) Regulations, 2024

SAINT CHRISTOPHER AND NEVIS
STATUTORY RULES AND ORDERS
No. 21 of 2024
Saint Christopher and Nevis Citizenship
(Marriage and Child Applications) Regulations, 2024

In exercise of the power conferred by section 15 of the Saint Christopher and Nevis
Citizenship Act, Cap. 1.05, the Minister makes the following Regulations
[Published 19th July 2024, Extra-Ordinary Gazette No. 41 of 2024]

## PART I

PRELIMINARY

### 1.

Citation.

These Regulations may be cited as the Saint Christopher and Nevis Citizenship (Marriage
and Child Applications) Regulations, 2024.

### 2.

Interpretation.
In these Regulations—
“Act” means the Saint Christopher and Nevis Citizenship Act, Cap. 1.05;
“authenticated translation” means a translation—
(a) prepared by a professional translator who is officially accredited by a court
of law, a government agency, an international organisation, or similar
official institution; or
(b) if done in a country where there are no official accredited translators, a
translation done by a company whose role or business is preparing
professional translations into the English language;
“child” means a biological or legally adopted child of a citizen of the Federation of
Saint Christopher and Nevis;
“child applicant” means a person who applies for citizenship under section 92(1)(g)
of the Constitution of Saint Christopher and Nevis;
“child application” means an application for citizenship under section 92(1)(g) of
the Constitution of Saint Christopher and Nevis;
“XCD” refers to Eastern Caribbean Dollars or the equivalent amount in any currency
that is legal tender in Saint Christopher and Nevis;
“Minister” means the Minister responsible for national security;

“Ministry” means the Ministry responsible for national security;
“police certificate” means an authorised statement from a national law enforcement
authority on the status of a person’s criminal record;
“spousal applicant” means a person who applies for citizenship under section
92(1)(a) of the Constitution of Saint Christopher and Nevis;
“spousal application” means an application for citizenship under section 92(1)(a)
of the Constitution of Saint Christopher and Nevis;
“spouse” means the husband or wife of a citizen of Saint Christopher and Nevis of
the opposite sex in accordance with the Marriage Act, Cap. 12.09, and the
Marriage (Prohibited Degrees of Relationship) Act of Saint Christopher and
Nevis, Cap. 12.10.

### 3.

Minister’s Authority.

(1) The Minister shall be responsible for approving or denying a spousal application
or a child application.
(2) In the exercise of his authority pursuant to sub-regulation (1), the Minister shall
consult with the Permanent Secretary of the Ministry.

## PART II

MARRIAGE VERIFICATION OFFICERS

### 4.

Appointment and functions of Marriage Verification Officers.

(1) The Minister shall appoint Marriage Verification Officers from persons employed
within the Ministry.
(2) Marriage Verification Officers shall be responsible for advising the Permanent
Secretary in the Ministry on all relevant facts pertaining to applications under these
Regulations including the following matters—
(a) the bona fides or legitimacy of any marriage which is the subject of a spousal
application;
(b) whether the spousal applicant has entered into a marriage for the primary purpose
of obtaining citizenship;
(c) whether the spousal applicant is a party to the marriage upon which the
application for citizenship has been made but—
(i) the parties to the marriage have separated;
(ii) there is a deed of separation in respect of that marriage;
(iii) divorce proceedings are pending in court in respect of that marriage;
(iv) whether the marriage has been terminated by divorce;
(v) the marriage has been annulled;
(vi) annulment proceedings are pending in court in respect of that marriage; or
(vii) the marriage is otherwise no longer subsisting;

(d) whether the spousal applicant is the spouse of a person who obtained citizenship
under section 3(5) of the Act;
(e) any past or pending legal proceedings, including domestic violence or criminal
proceedings between the parties to the marriage; or
(f) other related matters.
(3) A Marriage Verification Officer may, in connection with the carrying out of his or
her functions—
(a) interview the spousal applicant and his or her spouse, together or separately;
(b) conduct random checks on the spousal applicant and his or her spouse at their
matrimonial property or wherever they reside in Saint Christopher and Nevis;
(c) consult and seek the advice of, or other information from, such persons or
bodies whether inside or outside Saint Christopher and Nevis as they consider
appropriate;
(d) report to the Permanent Secretary in the Ministry on any findings obtained
from interviews, random checks or consultations;
(e) make recommendations arising out of any information received and advise the
Permanent Secretary accordingly; and
(f) any other related actions related to his or her statutory responsibilities.

### 5.

Gazetting of Marriage Verification Officers.

The appointment and cessation of appointment of any Marriage Verification Officer
shall be notified in the Official Gazette.

## PART III

QUALIFICATIONS, GENERAL REQUIREMENTS AND PROCEDURES FOR
SPOUSAL APPLICATIONS

### 6.

Qualifications for Spousal Applicants.
Any person who —
(a) is at least eighteen years of age; and
(b) is a spouse of a citizen of Saint Christopher and Nevis;

may apply as a spousal applicant to become a citizen of Saint Christopher and Nevis pursuant
to section 92(1)(a) of the Constitution of Saint Christopher and Nevis.

### 7.

Spousal Applications.

(1) Spousal applications shall be made to the Ministry on the forms provided by the
Ministry and accompanied by original documentation or certified copies of original documents
referenced in the forms.
(2) Spousal applications shall be submitted to the Ministry only on payment of a nonrefundable fee of one thousand dollars (XCD$1,000.00).
(3) A spousal application shall be accompanied by police record certificates which
must be obtained from all cities, states or countries, in which the spousal applicant resided
for three years prior to the submission of his or her application.

(4) The spouse of a spousal applicant shall provide a sworn affidavit of support for a
spousal application and, in that affidavit, the spouse shall make a statement that his or her
citizenship was not obtained by false pretence, fraud or other unlawful action.
(5) Each spousal application form must be completed personally by the spousal
applicant.
(6) Spousal applications shall only be accepted and processed by the Ministry if all
forms are properly completed, dated and signed, and if accompanied by all required documents
and fees.

### 8.

Interviews.

Every spousal application shall be examined by the Ministry and a Marriage Verification
Officer and, if deemed necessary, a spousal applicant may be requested to attend an interview
virtually, in person in Saint Christopher and Nevis, or in person at such other location approved
by the Minister.

### 9.

Initial Response to Spousal Applications.

(1) No later than two calendar years after the submission of a spousal application to
the Ministry, during which period the process of marriage verification by a Marriage
Verification Officer shall be conducted, the Ministry shall notify the spousal applicant
whether—
(a) the marriage has been provisionally verified, in consideration of the matters
contained in sub-regulation 4(2);
(b) the marriage has not yet been provisionally verified and more investigation is
required;
(c) the verification process has been delayed for cause; or
(d) the spousal application has been refused.
(2) Notwithstanding sub-regulation 1(d) and subject to regulation 10(2), the Ministry
may notify the spousal applicant of the refusal of the spousal application at any time after
the submission of an application.

### 10.

Post Marriage Verification Due Diligence.
(1) Before any decision is made in relation to a spousal application and before
citizenship can be granted to a spousal applicant, the Minister shall determine if the spousal
applicant shall undergo a due diligence background check.
(2) The Ministry or the Citizenship by Investment Unit shall commission any due
diligence background checks requested by the Minister.
(3) A commissioned independent professional firm shall conduct a due diligence
background check and submit a report to the entity which commissioned the report.
(4) The spousal applicant shall be notified of the cost of the due diligence background
check before it is commissioned, and that cost shall be borne by the spousal applicant.
(5) A due diligence background check may include—

(a) the secure collection and processing of biometric and other data including
fingerprints and passport verification; and
(b) the attendance of the spousal applicant for an interview virtually, in person in
Saint Christopher and Nevis, or in person at such other location approved by
the Minister.

### 11.

Final Marriage Verification and Decision
(1) No later than twenty-two months after the submission of a spousal application to
the Ministry and before citizenship can be granted to a spousal applicant, a Marriage
Verification Officer shall verify—
(a) whether—
(i) the parties to the marriage have separated;
(ii) there is a deed of separation in respect of that marriage;
(iii) divorce proceedings are pending in court in respect of that marriage;
(iv) the marriage has been annulled;
(v) annulment proceedings are pending in court in respect of that marriage; or
(vi) the marriage is otherwise no longer subsisting;
(b) whether there exists any pending legal proceedings, including domestic violence
or criminal proceedings between the parties to the marriage; or
(c) other related matters.
(2) No later than twenty-four months after the submission of a spousal application to
the Ministry, the Minister shall issue a decision with respect to the spousal application.

### 12.

False Statements by Spousal Applicants.
(1) Where a spousal applicant makes a false statement or omits information requested
on any of the forms, the spousal application may be refused.
(2) Where a spousal applicant or his or her spouse is subsequently found to have
provided false or incorrect information, the spousal applicant may be deprived of his or her
citizenship of Saint Christopher and Nevis pursuant to Section 8(a) of the Act titled
“Deprivation of citizenship obtained by registration” and may be prosecuted pursuant to the
provisions of the Perjury Act, Cap. 4.23.

## PART IV

QUALIFICATIONS, GENERAL REQUIREMENTS AND PROCEDURES FOR
CHILD APPLICATIONS

### 13.

Qualifications for Child Applicants.
An application may be submitted pursuant to section 92(1)(g) of the Constitution of
Saint Christopher and Nevis to register any child who is less than eighteen years of age as a
citizen of Saint Christopher and Nevis.

### 14.

Child Application Forms.
(1) The parent, guardian or legal representative of a child applicant shall, on the forms
provided by the Ministry, make an application to the Ministry for citizenship for that child

before the child applicant’s eighteenth birthday, and the application shall be accompanied
by original documentation or certified copies of original documents referenced in the forms.
(2) Child applications shall be submitted to the Ministry only upon payment of the
non-refundable application fee of one thousand (XCD1,000.00).
(3) A parent of a child applicant who is a citizen of Saint Christopher and Nevis shall
provide a sworn affidavit of support for that application and in that affidavit, the parent shall
make a statement that his or her citizenship was not obtained by false pretence, fraud or
other unlawful action.
(4) Child applications shall only be accepted and processed by the Ministry if all forms
are properly completed, dated and signed, and if accompanied by all required documents
and fees.
(5) Police record certificates accompanying child applications must be obtained from
all cities, states or countries, in which a child applicant who is sixteen years of age or between
sixteen and eighteen years, resided since he or she was sixteen years of age.

### 15.

Contents of the Child Application Form.
Each child application form must be signed—
(a) personally by the child applicant if he or she is sixteen years of age or between
sixteen and eighteen years old; or
(b) by his or her parent, guardian or legal representative if he or she is under
sixteen years of age.

### 16.

Interviews.
(1) A child application may be examined by the Ministry and, if deemed necessary, a
child applicant may be requested to attend an interview virtually, in person in Saint Christopher
and Nevis, or in person at such other location approved by the Minister.
(2) Where a child applicant is required to attend an interview, the child applicant shall
have his or her parent, guardian, or legal representative present during the interview.

### 17.

Due Diligence.
(1) Before any decision is made in relation to a child application and before citizenship
can be granted to a child applicant, the Minister shall determine if any child applicant sixteen
years of age or older, or his or her parents, shall undergo a due diligence background check.
(2) The Ministry or the Citizenship By Investment Unit shall commission any due
diligence background check requested by the Minister.
(3) A commissioned independent professional firm shall conduct the due diligence
background check and submit a report to the entity which commissioned the report.
(4) A parent of the child applicant shall be notified of the cost of the due diligence
background check before it is commissioned, and that cost shall be borne by a parent, guardian,
or legal representative of the child applicant.
(5) A due diligence background check may include—
(a) the secure collection and processing of biometric and other data including
fingerprints and passport verification; and

(b) the attendance of the child applicant or his or her parents for an interview
(i) in person in Saint Christopher and Nevis;
(ii) in person at such other location approved by the Minister; or
(iii) virtually on such terms and conditions as the Minister may approve.

### 18.

False Statements by Child Applicant or Parent.
(1) Where a child applicant or his or her parent makes a false statement or omits
information requested on any of the forms, the child application may be refused.
(2) Where a child applicant or his or her parent is subsequently found to have provided
false or incorrect information, the child may be deprived of his or her citizenship of
Saint Christopher and Nevis pursuant to Section 8(a) of the Act titled “Deprivation of
citizenship obtained by registration” and the parent may be prosecuted pursuant to the
provisions of the Perjury Act, Cap. 4.23.

## PART VI

MISCELLANEOUS

### 19.

Certified Copies.
Where a notary public or equivalently licenced person outside of Saint Christopher and
Nevis certifies a document to be a true copy of the original, that certification must be
independently authenticated by—
(a) in the case of the certification being conducted in countries that are parties to
the Hague Convention of 5th October 1961 Abolishing the Requirement of
Legislation for Foreign Public Documents, an Apostille in accordance with
the provisions of that Convention; or
(b) in the case of the certification being conducted in any country, a notarial or
equivalent certificate with the signature and stamp of the notary public or
equivalently licenced person licenced in that country who examined the original
document and made the true and exact copy, which shall contain the language
in Schedule 1 to these Regulations.

### 20.

Authenticated translations.
An application under these regulations shall be completed in the English language and
any document submitted with the application shall be in English, or, if the original language
of the document is not in English, the document shall be accompanied by an authenticated
translation.
