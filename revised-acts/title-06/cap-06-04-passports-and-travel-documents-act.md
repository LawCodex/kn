## CHAPTER 6.04

PASSPORTS AND TRAVEL DOCUMENTS ACT
AN ACT TO PROVIDE FOR THE ISSUING AND RENEWAL OF PASSPORTS AND TRAVEL
DOCUMENTS; AND TO PROVIDE FOR RELATED OR INCIDENTAL MATTERS.
Short title.

### 1.

This Act may be cited as the Passports and Travel Documents Act.

Interpretation.

### 2.

In this Act—

“Minister” means the Minister responsible for passports;
“overseas representative” means a diplomatic or consular representative of the
Government of Saint Christopher and Nevis in a foreign country;
“passport officer” means the Chief Passport Officer or a person designated by the
Minister pursuant to section 8;
“travel document” includes any visa, travel permit, certificate of identity or other
similar document issued by or on behalf of the Government of Saint
Christopher and Nevis.
Issue and renewal of passports and grant of visas, cancellation, etc.

### 3.

(1) The Minister may, in his or her discretion, issue passports in Saint
Christopher and Nevis to citizens of Saint Christopher and Nevis.
(2) Passports shall remain the property of the government and may be
cancelled or withdrawn at any time by the Minister.
(3) A passport so cancelled or withdrawn shall become void.
(4) The Minister may, in his or her discretion, renew passports and grant visas
on travel documents requiring visas to Saint Christopher and Nevis.
(Amended by Act 2 of 2012)

(5) Any renewal of a passport issued, or any visa granted, pursuant to
subsection (4) may be cancelled by the Minister.
(Amended by Act 2 of 2012)

(6) A cancellation of any renewal of a passport issued or any visa granted
shall render the renewal or visa void.
(Amended by Act 2 of 2012)

Power to issue and cancel permits, certificates of identity and travel documents.

### 4.

The Minister may, in his or her discretion, grant—
(a) a permit to leave Saint Christopher and Nevis to a person who is not a
citizen of Saint Christopher and Nevis; and
(b) certificates of identity and travel documents,

in such form as may be prescribed, and may cancel permits, certificates of identity
and travel documents so issued.

Powers of passport officers.

### 5.

Anything authorised under section 3 or 4 to be done by the Minister may be
done by the Chief Passport Officer or a passport officer authorised in that behalf by
the Minister.
Restrictions on validity.

### 6.

(1) The validity of a passport may be restricted to any named country or
countries as the Minister thinks fit.
(2) Subject to subsection (3), a passport may remain valid for a period of ten
years from the date of issue and no passport shall remain valid for more than ten
years from its date of issue.
(Substituted by Act 2 of 2012)

(3) A passport issued to a person sixteen years and under shall remain valid
for a period of five years and the validity of the passport shall not be extended for a
further period.
(Inserted by Act 2 of 2012)

Authorisation of overseas representatives to issue passports, etc.

### 7.

(1) The Minister may, from time to time, authorise an overseas representative
in any country in writing, to exercise in that country all or any of the powers that the
Minister is authorised by this Act to exercise in Saint Christopher and Nevis and in
every such case this Act shall, so far as it is applicable, apply with any necessary
modifications.
(2) In the exercise of powers pursuant to this Act, an overseas representative
shall act in accordance with such directions, general or special, as may be given to
him or her by the Minister.
(3) Any authorisation or directions under this section may be given to a
specified person or to persons of a specified class, or may be given to the holder or
holders for the time being of a specified office or appointment or class of offices or
appointments.
Designation of passport officers.

### 8.

(1) The Minister may designate passport officers for the purposes of this Act.

(2) The Permanent Secretary within the Ministry responsible for passports
shall be the Chief Passport Officer.
(Substituted by Act 9 of 2016)

(3) Officers so designated shall be subject to the directions of the Minister as
to the performance of their duties under this Act.
Regulations.

### 9.

The Minister may make regulations generally for carrying into effect the
purposes of this Act, and in particular, for the purpose of—
(a) prescribing anything which under this Act is required or permitted to
be prescribed;
(b) regulating the imposition and collection of fees for the issue and
renewal of passports, the grant of visas, permits, certificates of identity
and travel documents.

Offences and penalties.

### 10.

(1) A person who contravenes a direction given or any restriction, condition or
requirement imposed under this Act, or contravenes any regulation made under this
Act commits an offence.
(2) Every person who—
(a) alters or tampers with a passport;
(b) is found in unlawful possession of a passport;
(c) on finding a passport, neglects or fails, within seven days of the
finding, to send or give it to a passport officer or to the officer in
charge at the nearest police station;
(d) by impersonation or false representation holds himself or herself out as
being the holder or the rightful possessor of a passport when he or she
is not;
(e) by impersonation or false representation procures or attempts to
procure the issue of a passport to himself or herself or to any other
person;
(f) parts with the possession of a passport to another person without
lawful authority or for an unlawful purpose;
(g) destroys or makes away with a passport or by wilful neglect allows
any person to obtain possession of a passport;
(h) without having actual personal knowledge of the applicant for a
passport, makes a declaration verifying the declaration of the applicant
or vouching for the fitness of the applicant to receive a passport, or
makes such a declaration merely on information obtained,
commits an offence.
(3) A person who aids or abets any other person in the commission of an
offence against this Act, or who harbours a person whom he or she knows or has
reasonable grounds for believing to have committed an offence against this Act,
commits an offence.
(4) A person who is found guilty of an offence under this section is liable, on
summary conviction, to imprisonment for a period not exceeding three years or to a
fine not exceeding ten thousand dollars, and in default of payment of any fine, to
imprisonment for a period not exceeding one year.
(Amended by Act 2 of 2012)

Savings.

### 11.

The following Orders shall continue in force as if made under section 9 of this
Act—
(a) Passport (Exemption) Order, 1992, No. 10;
(b) Visa (Exemption) Order, 1985, No. 3.

## FIRST SCHEDULE

(Section 9)
PASSPORT REGULATIONS
Citation.

1\.

These Regulations may be cited as the Passport Regulations.

Interpretation.

2\.

In these Regulations—

“diplomatic passport” means a passport referred to in Regulation 4(a);
“official passport” means a passport referred to in Regulation 4(b);
“ordinary passport” means a passport referred to in Regulation 4(c);
“Minister” means the Minister responsible for Passports;
“spouse” means the husband or the wife of a person.

(Inserted by S.R.O. 63/2011)

Passport Applications.

3\.

All applications for a passport shall be made to the Chief Passport Officer.

Classes of Passport.

4\.

The Minister may, in his or her discretion, issue—
(a) a diplomatic passport to—
(i) a Governor-General (Past or Present) (including, upon request, a
spouse who travels on official duty);
(ii) a Deputy Governor-General (Past or Present) (including, upon
request, a spouse who travels on official duty);
(iii) a Prime Minister (Past or Present) (including, upon request, a
spouse who travels on official duty);
(iv) a Minister of Government (including, upon request, a spouse who
travels on official duty);
(v) a Speaker of the National Assembly (including, upon request, a
spouse who travels on official duty);
(vi) a Head or Deputy Head of the Diplomatic Mission of Saint
Christopher and Nevis in a foreign country;
(vii) a High Commissioner or Deputy High Commissioner of Saint
Christopher and Nevis;
(viii) a Consul or Consul General of Saint Christopher and Nevis;
(ix) a Permanent Secretary (upon request);
(x) a Head of Judiciary (if a national);
(xi) a Cabinet Secretary;
(xii) a Financial Secretary;

(xiii)Diplomats (including, upon request, a spouse or minors who
travel on duty with them overseas), namely—
(A)

Heads of Mission/Embassy/High Commission;

(B)

Deputy Head/Minister Counsellor;

(C)

Counsellor;

(D)

First Secretary;

(E)

Second Secretary;

(F)

Third Secretary;

(G)

Attaché;

(xiv) Heads of Consulates;
(xv) such other person as the Minister may designate from time to
time;
(Substituted by S.R.O. 63/2011)

(b) an official passport to—
(i) Heads of Department;
(ii) other officers on official duty at the level of assistant Secretary
and above;
(iii) any other Official as approved by the Minister;
(Substituted by S.R.O. 63/2011)

(c) an ordinary passport to any citizen of Saint Christopher and Nevis.
Application for Passport.

5\.
(1) No ordinary passport shall be issued unless an application and declaration
is made on the appropriate form specified in Schedule 2 to these Regulations and
available, on application, from a Passport and Visa Officer.
(2) Every application for a passport shall be made in the proper name of the
applicant.
(Amended by S.R.O. 63/2011)

Authentication and Sponsorship.

6\.
Each application for an ordinary passport shall be authenticated and sponsored
by a medical practitioner, a counsel and attorney of the Supreme Court, a member of
the National Assembly or Nevis Island Assembly, a Minister of religion, a senior
civil servant, a senior bank official, a gazetted police officer, a magistrate or justice of
the peace who has been personally acquainted with the applicant for at least two years
and who is not a member of the immediate family of the applicant.
Photographs required.

7\.
(1) Every application for an ordinary passport shall be accompanied by three
copies of a recent photograph of the applicant.
(Amended by S.R.O. 63/2011)

(2) Photographs shall be taken full face, without hat or other covering of the
head, and must not be mounted.
(Amended by S.R.O. 63/2011)

(3) The size shall not exceed two and one-half inches by two inches nor be
less than two inches by one and one-half inches.
(4) Photographs shall be coloured, printed on normal thin photographic paper
and they shall be left unglazed on the reverse side and shall be capable of adhesion to
a passport.
(Amended by S.R.O. 63/2011)

(5) The person authenticating and sponsoring the application shall endorse the
reverse side of one copy of each photograph with the words—
“I certify that this is a true likeness of the applicant (Mr, Ms, Mrs or Miss)”,
and shall add his or her signature.
\*

(6) All photographs which are appended to an application for a passport shall
become the property of the Government.
(Amended by S.R.O. 63/2011)

Countries for which Passports are valid.

8\. (1) A Passport shall be valid only for travel to the countries named therein but
may be endorsed for additional countries after issue, on payment of the requisite fee.
\**

(2) The form of a passport so endorsed cannot exempt the holder from
compliance with any immigration regulations in force in any country or from the
necessity of obtaining a visa wherever a visa may be required by the law of any such
country.
Fees.

9\.
(1) The fees specified in Schedule 1 to these Regulations shall be paid for the
matters specified in that Schedule.
(2) The Minister may in writing, determine the fees to be paid for the issue
and renewal of visas, passports and other travel documents overseas.
Validity of Passport.

10\. (1) Any passport may be renewed from the date of expiration for any further
period from one to five years at the request of the holder.
(2) Notwithstanding subsection (1), the validity of a passport shall not exceed
ten years from the original date of issue.
(Amended by S.R.O. 63/2011)

(3) After the expiration of ten years from the original date of issue a passport
shall not be capable of renewal and a new passport must be applied for.
(4) A new passport must be applied for whenever there is no further space for
the insertion of visas in a current passport.
Appearance of Applicant in person.

11\. (1) A Passport Officer, or in the case of an application outside Saint
Christopher and Nevis, a diplomatic agent or consular officer, may in his or her
discretion refuse to issue a passport unless the applicant appears in person.
Subregulation (6) is deleted and subregulation (7) is renumbered as subregulation (6)
Regulation 8 is deleted by S.R.O. 63/2011 and Regulations 9 to 13 are renumbered as
Regulations 8 to 12
\*

\**

(2) Notwithstanding sub-regulation (1), in the case of an application for the
replacement of a lost, stolen or damaged passport, an applicant shall be required to
appear in person before a new passport may be issued.
(Inserted by S.R.O. 63/2011)

(3) Pursuant to sub-regulation (2), a Passport Officer or in the case of an
applicant outside Saint Christopher and Nevis, a diplomatic agent or consular officer,
shall conduct a personal interview of the applicant to make a determination on
whether the applicant’s statement, together with or without the following documents
supporting the statement of the applicant, that his or her passport was lost, stolen or
damaged is sufficient evidence to justify the issuance of a new passport—
(a) police report;
(b) other official report; or
(c) other credible information.

(Inserted by S.R.O. 63/2011)

Custody of Official Passport.

### 12.

(1) The Chief Passport Officer shall be the custodian of all official passports.
(2) An official passport shall be given to the person to whom it is issued, not
more than five days before departure on an official trip, or for the purpose of
obtaining visa or other travel requirements in the country the official is visiting or
passing through.
(3) Subject to sub-regulation (4), a person who travels with an official
passport shall submit the passport to the Chief Passport Officer within five days of
returning to the Federation of Saint Christopher and Nevis, from an official trip.
(4) Notwithstanding sub-regulation (3), the holder of an official passport
serving in a diplomatic mission overseas shall submit his or her official passport to
the Chief Passport Officer only upon being recalled or at the end of his or her duty
tour overseas.
(5) No person shall use an official passport for a purpose that is not official.
(6) The Chief Passport Officer shall keep record of the movements of all
official passports.
Lost, stolen or damaged passports.
13
(1) The mandatory waiting time between application for and issuance of a
replacement passport for a lost, stolen or damaged passport shall be no less than 14
days except in circumstances where the applicant’s statement is supported by a police
report, or other official report.
(2) Subject to subregulation (4), where a person has lost two passports, that
person shall not be issued with a new passport until a period of ten years expires after
the person officially reports that the second passport is lost, stolen or damaged.
(3) A person who has been issued with a new passport after his or her passport
was reported as lost, stolen or damaged and who subsequently finds the old passport
or parts thereof shall present it to a Passport Officer for physical cancellation of the
passport.
(4) The provisions of subregulation (2) may only be waived in circumstances
where a full investigation of the matter indicates that the two passports were actually
lost, stolen or damaged and the issuance of a new passport is approved by the
Minister.
(Inserted by S.R.O. 63/2011)

## SECOND SCHEDULE

(Sections 9 and 11)
VISA (EXEMPTION) ORDER
Short title.

1\.

This Order may be cited as the Visa (Exemption) Order.

Visa exemption.

2\.
Aliens being citizens of the countries mentioned in the Schedule landing in
Saint Christopher and Nevis from any place beyond Saint Christopher and Nevis and
in possession of a valid passport issued to them by the proper authority of their
country of nationality shall be exempt from the provisions of the Immigration Act,
requiring passports of aliens to be visaed by a Saint Christopher and Nevis Consular
Officer or other person duly so authorised.

## THIRD SCHEDULE

(Sections 9 and 11)
PASSPORT (EXEMPTION) ORDER
Citation.

1\.

This Order may be cited as the Passport (Exemption) Order.

Passport Exemption.

2\.
The following persons are exempt from the provisions of the Immigration Act,
(which requires the possession of a valid passport by persons entering Saint
Christopher and Nevis)—
(a) Citizens of a country which is a Member State of the Caribbean
Community (CARICOM) as set out in the Schedule hereto and who
are in possession of a valid ticket issued by a steamship company or an
airline company providing for their return to the country to which they
belong and a form of identification bearing a photograph of the bearer,
the nationality and the date and place of birth of the bearer;
(b) Citizens of the United Kingdom, the United States of America or of
Canada who are visiting Saint Christopher and Nevis for a period not
exceeding six months and who are in possession of—
(i) a valid ticket issued by a steamship company or an airline
company providing for their return to the United Kingdom, the
United States of America or Canada respectively; and
(ii) a form of identification bearing a photograph of the bearer, the
nationality and the date and place of birth of the bearer;
(c) Passengers in transit by sea or air who do not intend to remain in Saint
Christopher and Nevis for more than fourteen days provided they are
in possession of—
(i) a through ticket to some other destination; and
(ii) a form of identification bearing a photograph of the bearer and the
nationality and the date and place of birth of the bearer;
(d) Officials of any of the following Organisations (hereinafter called “the
Organisation”)—
(i) The Caribbean Community;
(ii) The Organisation of Eastern Caribbean States;
(iii) The Organisation of American States;
(iv) The United Nations and its agencies,
who are visiting Saint Christopher and Nevis on official business of the Organisation
and who are in possession of an official travel document valid for travel to Saint
Christopher and Nevis in the form issued by the Organisation to its officials for travel
between Member States of the Organisation.
