## CHAPTER 7.10

PUBLIC PIERS AND WHARVES ACT
AN ACT TO MAKE PROVISION FOR THE LEVYING AND COLLECTION OF WHARFAGE RATES
ON GOODS, WARES, MERCHANDISE AND ANIMALS IMPORTED, EXPORTED, OR LANDED AT,
OR SHIPPED FROM ANY PIER, WHARF OR SHIPPING PLACE WITHIN THE STATE; AND TO
PROVIDE FOR RELATED OR INCIDENTAL MATTERS.
Short title.

### 1.

This Act may be cited as the Public Piers and Wharves Act.

Interpretation.

### 2.

In this Act—

“shipping place” means the shore or beach extending to a distance of one mile on
each side of any pier or wharf within the State, or such other distance as may
be fixed by resolution of the National Assembly;
“pier” or “wharf” means any pier or wharf in the State made subject to wharfage
rates, under this Act, by Resolution of the National Assembly.
Wharfage rates to be charged.

### 3.

(1) The rates as set forth in the First Schedule shall be charged, paid and
collected on all goods, wares, merchandise and animals imported or exported, or
landed at, or shipped from any pier, wharf or shipping place within the State save and
except upon—
(a) goods, wares, merchandise and animals going coastwise upon which
wharfage dues have already been paid;
(b) goods, wares, merchandise and animals exported or shipped as
aforesaid which have been landed in the course of transhipment and
upon which wharfage dues payable on the landing thereof have been
paid within one year prior to shipment.
(2) Except as provided in subsection (1), no person shall import, export, land
or ship, or attempt to import, export, land or ship any goods, wares, merchandise or
animals without having paid into the Treasury the rates charged thereon.
Application of wharfage rates.

### 4.

All moneys raised and collected under the provisions of this Act shall be paid
into the Treasury for the public uses of the State:
Provided always that, from and after the coming into operation of this Act the
cost of erecting and maintaining public piers and wharves shall be defrayed out of the
general revenue of the State.
Application of rules in the Second Schedule.

### 5.

The rules as set forth in the Second Schedule shall apply to all public piers,
wharves and shipping places situated within the State for the regulation and use of the
same, and for the government and control of the roadsteads thereof.

Penalties.

### 6.

Any person who violates or refuses or neglects to comply with any provisions
of this Act or of the rules set forth in the Second Schedule shall, on summary
conviction, be liable to a penalty not exceeding one hundred and fifty dollars.
(Amended by Acts 7 of 1976 and 9 of 1986)

Storing of goods in storehouses.

### 7.

All goods intended to be deposited in any storing house shall be stored in such
parts thereof and in such manner as the officer on duty shall direct, and all such goods
stored in any storing house as aforesaid shall be subject to the rentals as set forth in
the Third Schedule.
Piers, etc. under Government control.

### 8.

The piers, wharves, shipping places, roadsteads and storing houses situated
within the State shall be under the control and direction of the Government.
National Assembly to have power to amend and revise scale of rates and rules.

### 9.

The National Assembly may, by resolution, from time to time, as occasion
may require, alter, amend, add to, revise or rescind the scale of wharfage rates as set
forth in the First Schedule and also the Rules for the regulation and control of piers
and roadsteads as set forth in the Second Schedule and the rentals set forth in the
Third Schedule.

## SECOND SCHEDULE

(Section 5)
RULES FOR THE REGULATION AND CONTROL OF
PIERS AND ROADSTEADS
1\.
(1) The Comptroller of Customs shall control and direct the use of the several
shipping places, piers, tractors, trailers, cranes and other Government equipment in
such manner as may appear to him or her best adapted from time to time for the
loading and unloading of cargo.
(2) The Chief of Police shall be responsible for the maintenance of order and
the Comptroller of Customs shall ensure that the piers, tractors, trailers, cranes and
other equipment are maintained in efficient working condition.
(3) No person shall by himself or herself, his or her servants or agents use on
any pretence whatsoever any such tractor, trailer, crane or other Government
equipment without the permission of the Comptroller of Customs or some other
officer of the Customs Department designated by him or her for that purpose, and any
person to whom such permission shall be granted shall pay to the Comptroller of
Customs for the revenues of the State twenty dollars per hour or part thereof, for the
use of each mobile crane and ten dollars per hour or part thereof for the use of each
tractor:
(Amended by S.R.O.s 11/1974 and 67/1976)

Provided that the Comptroller of Customs may waive the payment of fees for
the use of any crane or tractor at any time whenever required for the purpose of
transporting from one island to any other island of the State any article being the
growth or produce of the State.
(Substituted by S.R.O. 12/1972)

2\.
(1) No idler shall be allowed on any pier during such time as goods or
passengers are being landed thereon or goods or passengers are being exported or
embarking therefrom and no females of notoriously bad character shall be allowed on
any pier at any other time.
(2) Beggars shall not at any time be permitted on any pier, and persons fishing
shall not at any time be permitted on the piers at Basseterre or Charlestown.
3\.
Any flat, boat or canoe which may be at the steps loading or discharging cargo
shall at once make way for any flat, boat or canoe approaching the steps for the
purpose of landing or embarking any passenger and luggage, if any, and such flat,
boat or canoe after landing such passenger shall at once make way for the flat, boat or
canoe which made way for it.
4\.
(1) All passengers, on landing, shall pass from the steps to the platform of the
piers or wharves, so soon as may be, and all luggage and goods landed, shall at once
be ranged with the least obstruction possible on one side of the platform, from which
they must be removed within a reasonable time.
(2) This rule applies conversely to passengers’ luggage and goods going off in
flats, boats or canoes.

5\.
On the arrival of any mail steamer and during the time the boat or boats of
such steamer may be at the pier or wharf, and on such other occasions as may be
deemed necessary by the Chief of Police, the piers or wharves shall be kept clear of
all persons, excepting those who may have business with any persons landing or
embarking at the time, and any officials whose presence may be necessary in the
discharge of any public duty:
Provided that licensed boatmen and porters decently clothed, and wearing their
badges as directed by law, shall have access to the piers and wharves subject to the
permission of the police, and provided further that any person who shall fight, quarrel
or make any disturbance shall at once be removed by the police.
6\.
No vessel shall make fast to any part of any pier or wharf without permission
being first obtained, and no flat, boat or canoe shall be made fast in front of the steps
on end of the pier or wharf except as prescribed in Rule 4.
7\.
All flats, boats or canoes which require to land their cargoes at any pier or
wharf shall do so in accordance with their order of arrival, and if any flat, boat, or
canoe from any cause is unable to continue discharging its cargo it shall at once give
place to the next in order of arrival.
8\.
All goods landed from flats, boats or canoes shall be at once ranged in order
on the pier or wharf at which they are landed with the least possible obstruction to the
general traffic, and shall be removed without delay.
9\.
All articles to be shipped from any pier or wharf shall be ranged in order with
the least obstruction possible on such pier, or in any adjoining street, and shall be
shipped according to the order of arrival of the flats, boats or canoes into which they
are to be put. Any flat, boat or canoe unable to continue taking cargo shall at once
make room for the next in order of arrival.
10\. A clear passage of not less than four feet shall at all times be kept in the centre
of every pier from end to end.
11\. No imported goods, wares or merchandise of any description except pitch
pine, white pine, and spruce pine lumber or shingles, shall be landed or deposited on
the beach or the open space lying between the two Basseterre piers and the public
street, and any such lumber or shingles imported, and so landed or deposited, shall
forthwith be removed, but hardwood, hogsheads, bricks, tiles, sugar pans, boilers and
other bulky articles imported into the State, may remain on other portions of the
public beach, or on the land adjoining thereto, not being a public street, for a period
not exceeding six weeks.
12\. No boats except those belonging to the Treasury, or under the care or custody
of the officers of the Treasury, shall be hauled up, nor shall any articles be placed on
the beach to the south of the Treasury buildings.
13\. No ballast or rubbish shall be placed on the bay on the space between the
streets by Adlam’s and France’s or Liverpool Row, nor any boats or vessels be
hauled up on the said space except jobbing boats in daily use.
14\. No wheeled vehicle of any kind shall be used or allowed on the piers, or under
the covered way of the Treasury, without the permission of the officer in charge.
15\. The piers and the approaches thereto shall be kept clear of all obstructions, and
all articles landed on the piers shall be at once removed.
16\. All machinery and other heavy articles, including hogs-heads and puncheons,
landed on or shipped from the piers, shall be moved on the tramway truck and in no
other manner.

17\. No decked or other heavy boat shall remain alongside the piers except while
actually loading, unloading, landing or embarking passengers and their luggage, and
when so engaged shall be removed to a sufficient distance so as not to obstruct the
piers.
18\. In cases of dispute arising under the above rules reference shall be made to the
Chief of Police or to any officer of the Treasury.
