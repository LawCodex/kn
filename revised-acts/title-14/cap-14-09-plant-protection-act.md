## CHAPTER 14.09

PLANT PROTECTION ACT
AN ACT TO MAKE PROVISION FOR THE PROTECTION OF PLANTS AGAINST CERTAIN
DISEASES; AND TO PROVIDE FOR RELATED OR INCIDENTAL MATTERS.
Short title.

### 1.

\*†

This Act may be cited as the Plant Protection Act.

Interpretation.

### 2.

In this Act—

“animal organism” means any animal organism in whatever stage of existence such
animal organism may be;
“Chief Agricultural Officer” means the Chief Agricultural Officer of the Ministry of
Agriculture or any Agricultural Officer authorized by him or her to exercise
the powers and discharge the duties of the Chief Agricultural Officer under
this Act;
(Inserted by Act 7 of 1976)

“disease” means any disease affecting trees, shrubs or herbs caused by or consisting
in the presence of any pathogenical animal or vegetable organism which the
Minister may by proclamation declare to be a disease within the meaning of
this Act;
“diseased” means affected with disease;
“inspection grounds” means such lots of land as may from time to time be set apart
by the Minister for the purposes of this Act;
“Minister” means the Minister responsible for Agriculture;
(Inserted by Act 7 of 1976)

“nursery” means any land or premises whereon are grown or kept any trees, shrubs or
herbs intended for sale or distribution;
“occupier” means the person in actual occupation of any land or building, or if there
is no such person, then the expression means the person entitled to possession
thereof;
“owner” means the person who is for the time being entitled to receive the rent of any
land, or who if the same were let to a tenant at a rack rent would be entitled to
receive such rent;
“pest” includes any parasitical, epiphytal or other animal or vegetable organism, and
also any insect or other invertebrate animal (in whatever stage of existence
such insect or animal may be) affecting or injurious to trees, shrubs or herbs,
which the Minister may by proclamation declare to be a pest within the
meaning of this Act;
“tree,” “shrub” and “herb” respectively include the fruit or any product of any tree,
shrub or herb and the roots, trunks, stems, branches, fruits, leaves or other

parts thereof or any parts, severed from any tree, shrub or herb, including
emptied pods or husks;
“vegetable organism” means any vegetable organism in whatever stage of existence
such vegetable organism may be.
(Amended by Act 7 of 1976)

Inspection grounds.

### 3.

The Minister may rent or lease land for the purposes of this Act, and may set
apart such land and also any Government land for use as inspection grounds or for
any other purpose which he or she may deem necessary to further the objects of this
Act.
(Amended by Act 6 of 1976)

Prohibition of importation of plants, etc.

### 4.

(1) The Minister may, by Notice, prohibit the importation into the State of any
plants, parts of plants, seeds, fruits, soil, or any class of goods, packages, coverings,
or other articles or things named and specified in such Notice.
(2) The Minister may, by Notice, order that the plants, parts of plants, seeds,
fruits, soil, or any class of goods, packages, coverings, or other articles or things
named or specified in such Notice shall not be imported into the State except upon
such conditions as may be specified in such Notice.
(3) Every plant, article or thing named in any Notice in force under this Act
imported into the State shall be deemed to have come from a place the introduction
thereof from which is prohibited by this Act and may be treated accordingly, unless
the importer satisfies the Comptroller of Customs or the principal Treasury or
Revenue Officer at the place of arrival, to the contrary.
(Amended by Act 7 of 1976)

Seizure.

### 5.

Every plant, article or thing imported into the State in contravention of any
Notice under this Act, or of any of the conditions imposed thereby may be seized by
any Treasury or Revenue Officer, by the Chief Agricultural Officer or by any
member of the Police Force and shall thereupon become forfeited to the Crown and
may be destroyed or otherwise dealt with as the Minister may direct.
(Amended by Act 7 of 1976)

Registration of Nurseries.

### 6.

Every nursery shall be registered by the owner or occupier thereof at the office
of the Chief Agricultural Officer on or before the 1st day of April in each year.
(Amended by Act 7 of 1976)

Inspection of nurseries.

### 7.

Every nursery shall be inspected at least twice a year by the Chief Agricultural
Officer or an Agricultural Officer appointed by him or her to fulfil that duty, and at
such other times as the Chief Agricultural Officer shall deem necessary.
(Amended by Act 7 of 1976)

Quarantine nurseries.

### 8.

The Chief Agricultural Officer may declare under quarantine any nursery or
such part of any nursery as lies within twenty yards of any tree, shrub or herb which

he or she finds infected by disease, or infested with pests, or within thirty yards if
such infected or infested tree, shrub or herb is over ten feet in height; and such area
shall be held and deemed to be under quarantine accordingly.
(Amended by Act 7 of 1976)

Quarantine of nurseries – contravention of.

### 9.

A quarantine declared under the last preceding section shall remain in force
until the Chief Agricultural Officer shall have given to the owner or occupier of the
nursery affected his or her written certificate to the effect that the trees, shrubs and
herbs within such quarantined area are free from disease and pests, and during such
period of quarantine it shall not be lawful for any person to remove or cause to be
removed from such nursery any tree, shrub or herb or any parts thereof or any soil,
manure, plant cases, wrappings or packing materials from the quarantined area.
(Amended by Act 7 of 1976)

Examination of quarantined nurseries.

### 10.

Any owner or occupier of a nursery having his or her nursery or any part
thereof thus placed under quarantine may, upon application to the Chief Agricultural
Officer, have his or her nursery examined free of charge to determine whether or not
he has been successful in eradicating the disease or pests.
(Amended by Act 7 of 1976)

Power to enter Plantations.

### 11.

The Chief Agricultural Officer may, at all reasonable times, enter without
notice (with or without assistants as may to the person making entry seem fit) upon
any nurseries, land or plantations or into any building or part of any building not
being a dwelling-house for the purpose of searching for and examining any animal or
vegetable organism or any diseased or pest-infested tree, shrub or herb or any soil,
manure, plant cases, wrappings or packing materials, and may remain thereon or
therein so long as may be reasonably necessary for any such purpose.
(Amended by Act 7 of 1976)

Procedure for eradication of diseases and pests.

### 12.

The Chief Agricultural Officer may issue an order in writing in the form set
out in the Schedule to this Act to the occupier, or if there be no occupier, to the owner
of any nursery, land or plantation, directing him or her to take within a definite time
from the service upon him or her of such order to be specified therein all such
measures as may be necessary for the eradication of or for the prevention of the
spread of any disease or pest, including in such measures the total destruction if
necessary of any trees, shrubs or herbs whether infected by any disease or infested
with any pests or not.
(Amended by Act 7 of 1976)

Procedure when occupier is absent.

### 13.

If there is no occupier on any land, service of any order under this Act may be
made by affixing the same in some conspicuous part of the land.
Non-compliance with order.

### 14.

If any owner or occupier fails to comply with the directions contained in any
order, the Chief Agricultural Officer may, with or without assistants, enter upon such

land and take such steps as may be necessary for properly and effectively carrying
such order into execution.
Expenses.

### 15.

The expenses incurred by the Chief Agricultural Officer in carrying into effect
any order made under this Act shall be paid by the party in default, and such expenses
shall be recoverable summarily as a civil debt in the manner provided by the
Magistrate’s Code of Procedure Act, Cap. 3.17:
Provided that the Minister may dispense with the payment of such expenses in
cases where, owing to the party in default being in needy circumstances, or for any
other reason, the Minister thinks fit so to do.
(Amended by Act 7 of 1976)

Compensation.

### 16.

The Minister may, out of moneys voted for that purpose by the National
Assembly, make grants by way of compensation or partial compensation to occupiers
and to owners (according to their respective interest) in respect of healthy trees,
shrubs or herbs, destroyed in order to prevent the increase of any disease or pest into
adjoining lands.
(Amended by Act 6 of 1976)

Protection of persons acting under Act.

### 17.

The Chief Agricultural Officer or other person authorized under the provisions
of this Act shall not be deemed a trespasser by reason of any entry or destruction or
action taken or thing done under this Act, or be liable for any damages occasioned by
carrying out any of the provisions of this Act, unless the same were occasioned
maliciously and without reasonable and probable cause.
(Amended by Act 7 of 1976)

Informality not to invalidate proceedings.

### 18.

Proceedings taken under this Act shall not be rendered void by reason of any
informality in any order or notice made or given.
Offences.

### 19.

A person commits an offence against this Act who—
(a) in any manner obstructs or impedes any person in the execution of any
of the powers conferred by this Act;
(b) refuses or neglects to comply with or acts in contravention of any of
the provisions of this Act or the terms of any proclamation issued
thereunder or any regulations made or any order given in pursuance
thereof respectively; or
(c) introduces any disease or pest into any cultivation, unless he or she
proves to the satisfaction of the Magistrate that such introduction was
accidental and not due to neglect or malice.

Penalties.

### 20.

(1) If any person is found guilty of an offence against this Act he or she shall
for every such offence be liable, on summary conviction, to a penalty not exceeding
five hundred dollars.
(Amended by Acts 7 of 1976 and 9 of 1986)

(2) The liability shall be in addition to the liability for civil claims which may
be made against such person.
Regulations and saving.

### 21.

(1) The Minister may make Regulations for all or any of the following
purposes, namely—
(a) for regulating or prohibiting the removal from any nursery, inspection
ground, plantation or place or the transference or conveyance from any
part of the State to any other part thereof of any trees, shrubs or herbs,
soil, manure, straw or packing materials;
(b) for securing the detention and examination of any tree, shrub or herb
and of any soil, manure, straw or packing material, which is suspected
to be diseased or pest-infested and of boxes, baskets, pots, packages
and cases which are suspected to contain or to have contained diseased
or pest-infested trees, shrubs or herbs or to harbour or to be infected
by disease or infested with pests;
(c) for securing the effectual treatment of diseased trees, shrubs and herbs
and the effectual eradication of diseases and pests, and for the supply
of chemicals, appliances and other means whereby such treatment or
eradication may be facilitated;
(d) for defining the duties of the Chief Agricultural Officer under this Act;
(Amended by Act 7 of 1976)

(e) for regulating the work to be done in inspection grounds;
(f) for regulating the manner in which requests are to be made by
occupiers to the Chief Agricultural Officer and the nature of such
requests; and
(g) generally for carrying into effect the provisions of this Act.
(2) Until and except in so far as they are cancelled by any proclamations to be
made under the authority of this Act the regulations, orders and proclamations with
reference to the matters aforesaid in force at the time of the commencement of this
Act shall continue to be in force after the passing of this Act.

## SECOND SCHEDULE

(Section 21)
MOSAIC DISEASE REGULATIONS
Short title.

1\.

These Regulations may be cited as the Mosaic Disease Regulations.

Suspected Existence of Disease to be Reported.

2\.
The owner or occupier of any land, plantation or estate who shall suspect the
existence of the Mosaic Disease upon the land, plantation or estate which is owned or
occupied by him or her shall at once inform the Chief Agricultural Officer thereof.
Chief Agricultural Officer to Visit Suspected Place.

3\.
Whenever the Mosaic Disease exists or is suspected to exist upon any land,
plantation or estate the Chief Agricultural Officer shall at once visit the locality
where the disease exists or is suspected to exist, and shall inform the owner or
occupier of his intention so to do:
Provided that this regulation shall in no way limit the authority of the Chief
Agricultural Officer under sections 7 and 11 of the Plant Protection Act.
Duty of Owner of Suspected Estate.

4\.
The owner or occupier of any land, plantation or estate who is informed of an
intention to visit the land, plantation or estate which he or she owns or occupies shall
arrange to meet the Chief Agricultural Officer upon the spot to be inspected, either by
himself or herself or by some responsible person (competent to receive and carry out
instructions for eradicating and preventing the spread of the disease.).

## THIRD SCHEDULE

(Section 4)
NOTICE PROHIBITING THE IMPORTATION OF FRUIT FROM
THE UNITED STATES OF AMERICA.
WHEREAS by subsection (1) of section 4 of the Plant Protection Act,
provision is made whereby the Minister may from time to time by Notice prohibit the
importation into the State of any plants, parts of plants, seeds, fruits, soil or any class
of goods, packages, coverings, or other articles or things named and specified in such
proclamation:
AND WHEREAS by subsection (2) of section 4 of the above mentioned
Act provision is made whereby the Minister may, from time to time, by Notice order
that the plants, parts of plants, seeds, fruits, soil, or any class of goods, packages,
coverings, or other articles or things named or specified in such proclamation shall
not be imported into the State except upon such conditions as may be specified in
such proclamation.
AND WHEREAS following a report of an occurrence of Mediterranean
Fruit Fly in the State of Florida in the United States of America it has been decided to
adopt measures as hereinafter provided affecting the importation of fruit into the State
from the United States of America:
NOW, THEREFORE, it is hereby ordered and notified as follows—
Absolute Prohibition.

1\.
Notwithstanding any of the provisions of a Proclamation dated the 10th day of
November, 1938, the importation into the State of any fruit from the State of Florida
in the United States of America is absolutely prohibited.
Conditional Prohibition.

2\.
Notwithstanding any of the provisions of the Proclamation dated the 10th day
of November, 1938, the importation into the State of any fruit imported from any part
of the United States of America other than the State of Florida is prohibited except
upon the authority of a phyto sanitary certificate from a responsible authority in such
part of the United States of America.
Definition.

3\.

For the purposes of this Notice—

“fruit” means any edible part or product derived from a flower but does not include
nuts, cereals and pulses.

## FIFTH SCHEDULE

(Section 2)
NOTICE DECLARING GUMMING DISEASE AND MOSAIC DISEASE OF
SUGAR CANE TO BE “DISEASES” WITHIN THE MEANING OF THE PLANT
PROTECTION ACT.
WHEREAS by virtue of section 2 of the Plant Protection Act it is provided
that the expression “disease” means any disease affecting trees, shrubs or herbs
caused by or consisting in the presence of any pathogenical animal or vegetable
organism which the Minister may by Notice from time to time declare to be a disease:
AND WHEREAS it is expedient in the interest of the sugar industry in the
State to declare that the diseases affecting sugar cane and known as Gumming disease
or Gummosis and Mosaic disease shall be declared to be a disease within the
meaning of the said Act:
NOW THEREFORE by virtue of the authority in me vested in that behalf I
do hereby declare that the diseases affecting sugar canes and called or known by the
name of Gumming Disease or Gummosis and Mosaic disease shall be diseases within
the meaning of the Act and I declare the same to be diseases accordingly.

## SIXTH SCHEDULE

(Section 2)
NOTICE DECLARING THE YELLOW DODDER (LOVE VINE)
PARASITE TO BE A PEST.
WHEREAS by virtue of section 2 of the Plant Protection Act it is ordained
that the expression “pest” includes any parasitical epiphytal or other animal or
vegetable organism, and also any insect or other invertebrate animal (in whatever
stage of existence such insect or animal may be) affecting or injurious to trees, shrubs
or herbs, which the Minister may by proclamation from time to time declare to be a
pest;
AND WHEREAS it is expedient to declare that the pest known as Yellow
Dodder or Love Vine shall be declared to be a pest within the meaning of the said
Act;
NOW THEREFORE by virtue of the authority in me vested in that behalf I
do hereby declare that the pest called or known by the name of Yellow Dodder or
Love Vine shall be a pest within the meaning of the said Act and I declare the same to
be a pest accordingly.
