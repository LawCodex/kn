## CHAPTER 14.08

LOCAL FOOD PRODUCTION ACT
AN ACT TO PROVIDE FOR THE PLANTING AND CULTIVATION OF VEGETABLES; TO
PROVIDE FOR THE KEEPING AND REARING OF LIVESTOCK; AND TO PROVIDE FOR RELATED
OR INCIDENTAL MATTERS.
Short title.

### 1.

This Act may be cited as the Local Food Production Act.

Interpretation.

### 2.

In this Act—

“arable land” means all land which is suited or which in the opinion of the Chief
Agricultural Officer may, by cultivation, be made suited to the growing of
vegetables;
“Chief Agricultural Officer” means the officer for the time being in charge of the
Agricultural Department of the State of Saint Christopher and Nevis;
“crop year” means the period beginning on the 1st day of January in any year and
ending on the 31st day of December in the same year;
“grassland” means all land which sustains as a natural growth flora, grass or other
herbaceous or low scrub plants, and includes all land which is normally used
for grazing animals, or which may be so used although planted with trees, or
otherwise cultivated;
“livestock” means all animals whose carcasses or products are fit for consumption by
man, and includes cattle, sheep, goats, pigs and poultry;
“owner” means every person having for the time being possession, charge or control
of any plantation, and includes every attorney, manager or agent of an owner
as well as tenants;
“plantation” means a holding of not less than ten acres of arable land or grassland or
of a combination of both;
“vegetables” includes sweet potatoes, yams, eddoes, corn, cassava, peas, beans, and
all other trees, plants, roots, fruits or other agricultural produce which are used
or are capable of being used, whether after treatment or otherwise, as food, or
as an ingredient in food, for man.
Returns to be furnished by plantation owners.

### 3.

(1) Every owner of a plantation shall, within thirty days of the coming into
operation of this Act, and thereafter whenever required so to do by Notice issued by
the Chief Agricultural Officer, furnish the Chief Agricultural Officer with a return
setting out—
(a) the acreage of his or her plantation;
(b) the total arable acreage of his or her plantation;
(c) the total acreage of sugar cane, cotton and coconut cultivation on his
or her plantation;
(d) the total acreage of grassland on his or her plantation;

(e) the total acreage of forest land on his or her plantation;
(f) the name (if any), acreage and locality of the fields which, on the date
on which the return is made, are planted with any vegetables, and, if
there be more than one kind of vegetable, the acreage planted with
each kind, and the date or dates on which each kind was planted;
(g) the kind of livestock kept on the plantation and the number of each
kind;
(h) the total acreage of the plantation under peasant or worker cultivation
on the 1st December, 1966; and
(i) the total acreage of the plantation under peasant or worker cultivation
on the date when the return is made.
(2) Any person who fails to comply with the provisions of subsection (1) of
this section or who knowingly submits a return false in any material particular
commits an offence against this Act and shall be liable, on summary conviction, to a
fine not exceeding two thousand five hundred dollars or to imprisonment for a term
not exceeding six months or to both such fine and imprisonment.
(Amended by Act 9 of 1986)

Plantation owner to be required to plant and cultivate vegetables.

### 4.

(1) Every owner of a plantation, upon being required so to do by notice issued
by the Chief Agricultural Officer, shall during the crop year plant, cultivate and
harvest or cause to be planted, cultivated and harvested on not less than two and not
more than twenty per centum of the total arable acreage of his or her plantation,
vegetables of such kind and to such an extent as may be specified in the notice:
Provided that the acreage to be so planted in vegetables shall not include land
under peasant or worker cultivation on the 1st December, 1966.
(2) A notice under this section may be served at any time for the planting of
vegetables during the part of the crop year specified therein.
Plantation owner to be required to keep livestock.

### 5.

(1) Every owner of a plantation, upon being required so to do by notice issued
by the Chief Agricultural Officer, shall at the times and in the manner required keep
or cause to be kept on his or her plantation livestock of such a kind and in such
numbers as may be specified in the notice:
Provided that no bull, ram or boar required to be kept shall exceed, in the case
of a bull, two years, and in the case of a ram, or a boar, one year, except where such
animals are kept solely for breeding purposes.
(2) For the purposes of this section livestock shall not be deemed to be kept on
a plantation unless such livestock is wholly owned by the owner of the plantation.
Control of slaughter of livestock.

### 6.

(1) Except with the written permission of the Chief Agricultural Officer or of
an Agricultural Officer authorised by him or her in writing in that behalf, or of a
Government Veterinary Officer, no person shall slaughter or sell, offer for sale or
deliver for slaughter, and no person shall purchase or receive for slaughter—
(a) any bull, cow, heifer, sheep, pig or goat which is or which may
become fit for breeding; or

(b) any ox, wether sheep, wether goat or barrow pig, unless—
(i) the live weight of the same is at the least, in the case of an ox, six
hundred pounds, in the case of a wether sheep or wether goat,
seventy pounds, and in the case of a barrow pig, ninety pounds;
(ii) in the case of any ox, wether sheep or wether goat, the same
possesses a minimum of two permanent (broad) teeth; or
(iii) the same is injured to such extent that it cannot be expected to
thrive.
(2) No person shall, except by the written permission of the Chief Agricultural
Officer or of an Agricultural Officer authorised by him or her in writing in that behalf
or of a Government Veterinary Officer, render barren by operation any bull, cow, pig,
sheep or goat whose age is greater than three months.
(3) If the death of any bull, ox or heifer, which shall have died otherwise than
by slaughter permitted by the Chief Agricultural Officer or an Agricultural Officer
authorised by him or her in writing in that behalf or a Government Veterinary Officer
under the provisions of this section, shall have been due to injury wilfully inflicted by
the owner thereof or by any person, acting for him or her, or to the neglect of such
owner or person, or to starvation or malnutrition, then the owner of such bull, ox or
heifer, and any person acting for him or her in charge of such bull, ox or heifer,
commits an offence against this Act.
(4) Any person who infringes or fails to comply with any of the provisions of
this section commits an offence against this Act and shall be liable, on summary
conviction, to a fine not exceeding two thousand dollars.
(Amended by Act 9 of 1986)

Notices.

### 7.

(1) Every owner on whom any Notice under this Act is served shall comply
with the requirements of such Notice within the time specified in such Notice, and if
he or she fails so to do he or she, unless he or she shall proves that such failure was
not due to his or her own negligence or wilful default, commits an offence against
this Act and shall be liable, on summary conviction, to a fine not exceeding five
thousand dollars or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding six months; and for a
second or subsequent offence, to a fine not exceeding eight thousand dollars or to
imprisonment for a term not exceeding nine months or to both such fine and such
imprisonment.
(Amended by Act 9 of 1986)

(2) The notice may be served on an owner either personally or by being sent
through the post and shall in the latter case be deemed to have been served not later
than the sixth day succeeding the day when posted, and for proof of such service it
shall be sufficient to prove that the letter containing the Notice was properly
addressed and posted.
Power to inspect plantation.

### 8.

(1) Inspectors authorised by the Chief Agricultural Officer in writing in that
behalf may enter and inspect any plantation at any time between the hours of six
o’clock in the morning and six o’clock in the evening with a view to seeing whether
the requirements of this Act or any Notice issued in pursuance to the provisions of
this Act are being or have been obeyed.

(2) Any person who hinders, obstructs or molests, or attempts to hinder,
obstruct or molest, or causes to be hindered, obstructed or molested any Inspector
duly authorised by the Chief Agricultural Officer while in the performance of his or
her duties under this Act commits an offence against this Act and shall be liable, on
summary conviction, to a fine not exceeding two thousand dollars or to imprisonment
for a term not exceeding six months.
(Amended by Acts 9 of 1986)

Forms.

### 9.

The Forms set out in the Schedule to this Act, or forms to the like effect, may
be used in the cases to which they refer, with such variations as circumstances may
require.
