## CHAPTER 9.22

PUBLIC POUNDS ACT
AN ACT TO MAKE PROVISION FOR PUBLIC POUNDS; FOR THE APPOINTMENT OF POUND
KEEPERS; FOR THE IMPOUNDING OF ANIMALS ETC.; AND TO MAKE PROVISION FOR
RELATED OR INCIDENTAL MATTERS.
Short title.

### 1.

This Act may be cited as the Public Pounds Act.

Interpretation.

### 2.

In this Act—

“animal” means any horse, cattle, ass, mule, sheep, pig or goat;
“authorized person” means any person appointed by the Minister to shoot stray or
diseased animals and includes a police officer;
(Inserted by Act 21 of 2007)

“feathered stock” means any fowl, turkey, peafowl, guinea-bird, duck or goose;
“owner” includes any person who, with the consent of the owner, has the charge or
custody of any animal or feathered stock.
Public pounds.

### 3.

(1) The public pounds in use at the commencement of this Act shall continue
to be public pounds.
(2) The Minister responsible for Agriculture may, by Order, authorise the
establishment of new public pounds or abolish any existing public pounds.
(Amended by Act 6 of 1976)

Appointment of pound-keepers.

### 4.

(1) The Minister may, from time to time, appoint persons to be pound-keepers
for the purposes of this Act.
(2) The appointment of any person as a pound-keeper may be terminated at
any time by the Minister.
(3) Every pound-keeper shall be entitled to demand, and receive the fees
mentioned in the First Schedule, and such fees shall attach and become a primary lien
on animals impounded.
Act.

(4) The Minister may, by Statutory Order, amend the First Schedule to this
(Inserted by Act 11 of 1998)

Impounding of animals.

### 5.

(1) The owner or occupier of any land, or any person authorised by him or her,
may seize any animal found trespassing on such land and may send or take it to the
pound of the district in which such land or any part of it is situate, or to the nearest
pound, to be detained and dealt with according to the provisions of this Act:

Provided that—
(a) the owner or occupier of the land trespassed upon may secure and
keep any animal seized under this section before sending the same to
the pound for any time not exceeding two days after the day of
seizure; and
(b) where any animal has been seized under this section, if the owner of
such animal or any person authorised by him or her, at any time before
such animal is received into the pound, pays, for the use of the owner
or occupier of the land trespassed upon, to the person then having the
charge of such animal the seizure fee specified in the Second Schedule
hereto and the rates per mile for driving them according to the scale in
the Third Schedule, the person then having charge of such animal shall
upon such payment being made deliver the animal to the person
making the payment.
(Substituted by Act 10 of 1975)

(2) In every case in which any animal having the same owner shall be found
trespassing on the same land more than once, and any such trespass shall take place
within fourteen days after any preceding trespass, there shall be charged for the
second and every other such trespass twice the seizure fees set forth in the Second
Schedule hereto, together with rates for driving according to the Third Schedule
hereto.
(Inserted by Act 10 of 1975, and amended by Act 9 of 1983)

(3) The Minister may, by Statutory Order, amend the Second and Third
Schedules to this Act.
(Inserted by Act 11 of 1998)

Animals found in any public place.

### 6.

(1) If any animal is found tethered, straying, or lying in any public place, it
may be seized and sent or taken to a pound by any person finding the same.
(Amended by Act 8 of 1985)

(2) Where any sheep, pig, goat or cattle cannot be impounded under
subsection (1), it shall be lawful for any authorised person to shoot the said sheep,
pig, goat or cattle.
(Inserted by Act 8 of 1985 and amended by Act 21 of 2007)

(3) Where any animal has been shot under subsection (2), the authorised
person shall forthwith notify the Public Health Department of such shooting and shall
also where the animal shot is identified as the particular property of any person, give
notice to the owner within six hours of such identification.
(Inserted by Act 8 of 1985 and amended by Act 21 of 2007)

(4) Upon receipt of notice under subsection (3) the Public Health Department
shall make arrangements for the removal of the carcass to a suitable place to await its
being claimed by the owner and if within twenty-four hours after such removal the
identified carcass has not been claimed or forthwith if it is not identified, it shall be
considered as abandoned and shall be deemed the property of the Government and
shall be disposed of as the Public Health Department shall think fit.
(Inserted by Act 8 of 1985)

(5) An authorised person shall not be accountable to the owner of any animal
shot under the provisions of this section and shall not be liable to be sued by such
owner in respect of such shooting.
(Inserted by Act 8 of 1985 and amended by Act 21 of 2007)

Liability for animals in public places.

\*

### 7.

Where any damage whether direct or indirect is caused to any person or thing
as a result of any animal wandering, straying or lying in any public place,
notwithstanding the provisions of any other law, the owner of such animal shall be
liable for such damage unless he can prove, the onus of which proof is upon him or
her—
(a) that he or she did not know or did not suspect or that he or she did not
have any reason to know or to suspect that such animal had wandered,
strayed or was lying in any public place; and
(b) that he or she had taken all reasonable steps to prevent such animal
from wandering, straying or lying in any public place.
(Inserted by Act 8 of 1985)

Inquiries as to ownership of animals impounded.

### 8.

When any animal is brought to any pound it shall be the duty of the poundkeeper to make, and of the person bringing the animal to the pound to answer, all
such inquiries as may be likely to ascertain the owner thereof.
Notice to owner of animal impounded.

### 9.

Where any animal is impounded, the pound-keeper shall within twenty-four
hours serve notice of that fact upon the owner, if known, by leaving such notice at the
usual place of abode or business of the owner.
Notice where owner of animal not known.

### 10.

If, at the expiration of twenty-four hours after any animal has been impounded,
the pound-keeper is unable to ascertain who is the owner of the animal, the poundkeeper shall cause a notice of the impounding of the animal to be posted at some
conspicuous part of the pound and at any other place which the Chief of Police may
specify.
Pound-keepers to feed animals impounded.

### 11.

Pound-keepers shall provide for animals impounded all necessary food and
water.
Shooting of sheep, pigs, goats, cattle or feathered stock doing actual damage.

### 12.

(1) It shall be lawful for any proprietor, possessor or other person in charge of
any estate, land or property, or any person authorised by any proprietor, possessor or
other person in charge of any estate, land or property to shoot any sheep, pig, goat,
cattle or feathered stock found trespassing upon any such estate, land or other
property and doing actual damage thereon.
(2) Where any sheep, pig, goat, cattle or feathered stock is shot, if it is
identified as particular property, notice of such shooting shall be given by the
proprietor or possessor of land, or his or her agent or any person authorized by the
proprietor, possessor or other person in charge to the owner of the animal or feathered
stock within six hours of such identification.

Note: This section was previously section 6A and has been renumbered as section 7.The other
sections have been accordingly renumbered.

\*

(3) If the proprietor, possessor, agent or other person in charge fails to give
such notice he or she commits an offence and liable, on summary conviction, to a fine
of seventy-five dollars.
(4) Where the owner of such animal or feathered stock does not remove the
same within six hours after the receipt of the notice or if the animal or feathered stock
cannot be identified, it shall be considered as abandoned and be deemed the property
of the party aggrieved.
(5) Any person shooting any cattle or authorising the shooting of any animal
or feathered stock in accordance with this section shall not be accountable to the
owner of any animal or feathered stock shot and shall not be liable to be sued by such
owner in respect of such shooting.
(Substituted by Act 8 of 1985)

Disposal of impounded animals.

### 13.

Any animal received into any pound under this Act shall be detained until sold
under the provisions of this Act, unless before the time for such sale the seizure fee
specified in the Second Schedule and the mileage rates for driving or taking animals
to pound specified in the Third Schedule, where such fees and rates are payable, and
the pound fee specified in the Third Schedule, are paid.
(Amended by Act 10 of 1975)

Sale of impounded animals.

### 14.

(1) A pound-keeper may, on such days as the Chief of Police from time to
time may direct, put up for sale by public auction any animal which has been detained
for more than three days, exclusive of the days of seizure and sale, and may sell and
deliver such animal to the highest bidder for ready money and, if the amount is not
forthwith paid, may put up the animal again for sale, and such public notice of every
sale under this section shall be given as the Chief of Police may direct.
(2) Out of the moneys received from any sale under this section, the poundkeeper shall in the first place deduct the pound fees, and out of the surplus shall pay
the seizure fee to the person whose land was trespassed upon if and when he or she
attends and demands the same, and shall pay the balance of such surplus (if any) to
any person known to be the owner of the animal and who attends and demands such
balance:
Provided that if no person having authority to receive such surplus, or the
balance of such surplus, attends and demands the same before the expiration of the
day of sale, the pound-keeper shall forthwith pay such surplus or the balance thereof
to the Magistrate of the district in which the pound is situate, and the Magistrate shall
cause the same to be kept until the person or persons respectively entitled thereto
appear before him or her and prove their claims to the same.
(3) If at the expiration of six months after any sale under this section the
Magistrate still has any portion of the moneys received on account of such sale, he or
she shall forthwith pay the same into the Treasury for the use of the State, and all
right to the moneys so paid over shall thereupon be extinguished.
(Amended by Act 21 of 2007)

Destruction of animals by pound-keeper.

### 15.

(1) If an animal is offered for sale and no offer is made for it, or the offer
made is, in the opinion of the pound-keeper, trifling, or if the animal is diseased or is
in such a state or condition that it is unfit to be sold, it shall be lawful for the pound-

keeper, with the approval of the Chief of Police, to have such animal shot or
otherwise destroyed and the carcass buried or otherwise disposed of.
(2) The owner shall, in addition to any other fees to which he or she may be
liable, repay to the pound-keeper the costs, charges, and expenses attending such
shooting or destruction and burial or disposition.
(3) If a healthy animal is offered for sale, as is referred to in subsection (1) and
fails to be sold on that occasion, the pound-keeper may also direct that the animal be
slaughtered and sold and the proceeds from the sale shall be dealt with in that same
manner as if the live animal had been sold.
(Amended by Act 21 of 2007)

Books to be kept by pound-keepers.

### 16.

Every pound-keeper shall keep such books as the Chief of Police may from
time to time direct for the purpose of identifying the animals impounded, describing
the owners of the same, the persons bringing the same to the pound, specifying the
moneys due or received in respect of any animal and the disposal of all moneys
received by such pound-keeper, and registering such other matters as the Chief of
Police may from time to time direct.
Powers of entry and inspection.

### 17.

Any police officer or other person authorised in that behalf by the Chief of
Police shall, for the purposes of this Act, have power to enter any public pound and to
demand of the pound-keeper for inspection the production of any books required to
be kept under section 16, and to inspect any animals in the said pound.
Offences.

### 18.

Any person who—
(a) drives, leads, or entices any animal into any land or into any public
place with intent to impound such animal or to procure the same to be
impounded;
(b) rescues, or releases, or attempts to rescue or release, any animal while
in or being taken to any pound, or breaks, or injures any pound, or
does, or aids or abets the doing of, any act whereby any animal
impounded may escape, or be unlawfully liberated; or
(c) otherwise acts in contravention of or fails to comply with any of the
provisions of this Act or any directions given by the Chief of Police
under or in pursuance of this Act,

commits an offence against this Act and shall, on summary conviction, be liable to a
fine not exceeding four hundred dollars or to imprisonment with or without hard
labour for a term not exceeding three months.
(Amended by Acts 7 of 1976 and 9 of 1986)

Act not to affect right of action.

### 19.

The remedies given by this Act in respect of animals trespassing shall be in
addition to, and not in derogation of, any remedy by action or suit to which any
person may be entitled in respect of any such trespass.
