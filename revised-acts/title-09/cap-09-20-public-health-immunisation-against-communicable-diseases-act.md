## CHAPTER 9.20

PUBLIC HEALTH (IMMUNISATION AGAINST
COMMUNICABLE DISEASES) ACT
AN ACT TO PROVIDE FOR THE IMMUNISATION OF PERSONS AGAINST COMMUNICABLE
DISEASES; AND TO PROVIDE FOR RELATED OR INCIDENTAL MATTERS.
Short title.

---

### 1.

This Act may be cited as the Public Health (Immunisation against
Communicable Diseases) Act.
Interpretation.

### 2.

In this Act, unless the Context otherwise requires—

“certified assistant” means a person certified by the Chief Medical Officer under
section 3 to have the necessary skill and knowledge to immunise persons
against communicable diseases;
“child” means a person who is under twelve years;
“communicable disease” means any disease from time to time specified in the First
Schedule to this Act;
“Medical practitioner” means a person registered under the Medical Act, Cap. 9.15;
“Minister” means the Minister responsible for health;
“nursery school” means a school including a part of a school used mainly for the
purpose of providing education for children under five years;
“parent” means the father and mother of a child, and any person having the care and
custody of a child;
“prescribed area” means any district, area, or place in respect of which an order under
section 5 has been made and is in force;
“primary school” means a school including a part of a school used mainly for the
purpose of providing education for children under twelve years;
“successful immunisation” means an immunisation which is efficient to protect
against a communicable disease.
Medical practitioners and certified assistants.

### 3.

The following persons shall be qualified to immunise persons against
communicable diseases for the purposes of this Act—
(a) medical practitioners in the Government service;
(b) duly qualified medical practitioners not in the Government service
who shall be appointed by the Minister on the advice of the Chief
Medical Officer for the purposes of this Act; and
(c) such other persons appointed by the Minister for the purposes of this
Act from among persons certified by the Chief Medical Officer to
have the necessary skill and knowledge to immunise persons against
communicable diseases.

Immunisation to be free.

### 4.

It shall be the duty—
(a) of medical practitioners in the Government service;
(b) of such medical practitioners not in the Government service as may be
appointed by the Minister under section 3; and
(c) of such certified assistants as may be appointed by the Minister under
section 3,

to immunise, free of charge, such persons as shall present themselves, or be presented
for the purpose, or such persons as, under the provisions of this Act, are, or become
liable, to be immunised against any communicable disease, except in cases where, in
the opinion of any such medical practitioner or certified assistant, immunisation
would be injurious to health, or where in the case of any communicable disease
satisfactory evidence that any person liable to be immunised is already successfully
immunised against that disease or has suffered from such communicable disease
whereby immunity to it was derived.
Power of Minister to order compulsory immunization.

### 5.

(1) The Minister may, by Order published in the Gazette, either generally or
with reference to particular districts, areas or places throughout the State, or with
respect to any particular class or classes of persons and with respect to any
communicable disease or diseases, prescribe that all children to whom such Order
applies, who do not produce satisfactory evidence of successful immunisation against
any or all of such communicable diseases specified in the Order, or of having
suffered from all or any of the diseases, shall be immunised by a medical practitioner
or by a certified assistant unless, in the opinion of a medical practitioner,
immunisation would be injurious to health.
(2) An Order made under this section may be made subject to any
qualifications and exceptions, as to the Minister may seem proper, and the provisions
of section 7 shall be read subject to any such qualifications and exemptions.
Power of Chief Medical Officer to make rules.

### 6.

Subject to the approval of the Minister, the Chief Medical Officer shall give
directions with respect to—
(a) the supply of vaccine to medical practitioners and to certified
assistants;
(b) the method of immunisation by medical practitioners and certified
assistants;
(c) the treatment of persons after immunisation;
(d) the evidence to be accepted of successful immunisation;
(e) the times and places of attendance of persons including adults upon
medical practitioners and certified assistants.
Immunisation of children.

### 7.

The parent of any child to which an Order made under section 5 applies, shall,
within two years after the birth of such child, or if the child is over two years at the
date of such order, within such period as may be appointed under section 6, bring

7

such child to a medical practitioner or certified assistant, at the time and place
appointed under section 6, for examination, and, if necessary, for immunisation, and
shall subsequently produce such child at such times and places as the medical
practitioner or certified assistant shall direct for the purpose of examination as to
whether or not the immunisation has been successful, and, if necessary, for reimmunisation.
Certificate of successful immunization.

### 8.

Every medical practitioner and every certified assistant who has immunised
any adult or child, against a communicable disease, and has ascertained that the
immunisation has been successful, or is satisfied that such adult or child is
insusceptible of successful immunisation, shall issue a certificate in the form A or B
respectively of the Second Schedule, if at the time application is made to him or her
by such adult or the parent of such child.
Fees.

### 9.

It shall be lawful for the Minister, after consultation with the Minister of
Finance and subject to the approval of the Cabinet, to make regulations dealing with
the payment of fees, if any, to medical practitioners and to certified assistants for
services performed by them in pursuance of this Act and to fix the amount of such
fees.
Regulations.

### 10.

The Minister may make regulations generally for giving effect to the purposes
of this Act.
Penalties.

### 11.

(1) Subject to subsection (2) any person who fails to comply with any
provisions of this Act relating to the immunisation of children or of any Order or
regulation made thereunder relating to the immunisation of children or who wilfully
endeavours to deceive, by false statement or otherwise, or obstructs, any medical
practitioner or certified assistant in the discharge of his or her duties under this Act,
shall be liable, on summary conviction, to a fine not exceeding three hundred dollars,
or to imprisonment for six months and when the failure continues after conviction, to
a fine not exceeding ten dollars for each day on which such default continues.
(2) All persons having attained sixteen years shall be liable, on failure to
comply with any provision of this Act, to the penalties prescribed by subsection (1)
and in the case of a child not having attained that age the parent, guardian or person
having the custody of such child, or with whom such child may be residing, shall be
so liable.
(Amended by Act 9 of 1986)

Certificate of immunisation required for admission to nursery schools and primary
schools.

### 12.

No person may be admitted into any nursery school or primary school unless
he or she produces to the Principal thereof a certificate of immunisation with respect
to every communicable disease, save that where a person produces a certificate of a
medical practitioner certifying that immunisation against any particular
communicable disease or communicable diseases is not advisable on medical

grounds, no certificate of immunisation shall be required to be produced with respect
to that communicable disease or those communicable diseases, as the case may be.
Communicable diseases to which Act applies.

### 13.

(1) The communicable diseases to which this Act refers are those specified in
the First Schedule to this Act and such other diseases as the Minister may prescribe
by notice published in the Gazette.
(2) A notice made under this section shall be laid before the National
Assembly.
Minister’s power in time of epidemic.

### 14.

Where any communicable disease has become epidemic or when an epidemic
of any such disease is threatened, the Minister may, by Order, suspend from
attendance at any nursery school or primary school persons to whom a certificate of a
medical practitioner certifying that immunisation against any such disease is not
advisable on medical grounds, was issued pursuant to section 12 or refuse entry to
any such person to any such school, and the suspension or refusal of entry, as the case
may be, shall be effective until the Minister by further Order declares that the
epidemic is ended or the threat of such epidemic is past.
