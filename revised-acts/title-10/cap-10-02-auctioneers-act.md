## CHAPTER 10.02

AUCTIONEERS ACT
AN ACT TO MAKE PROVISION FOR THE LICENSING OF AUCTIONEERS; AND TO MAKE
PROVISION FOR RELATED OR INCIDENTAL MATTERS.
Short title.

### 1.

This Act may be cited as the Auctioneers Act.

Interpretation.

### 2.

Any person who—
(a) exercises or carries on the trade or business of an auctionee;
(b) acts in such capacity at any sale; or
(c) sells or offers for sale any goods or chattels, lands, tenements or
hereditaments, or any interest therein, at any sale where any person
becomes the purchaser of the same by competition and being the
highest bidder, either by being the single bidder or by increasing upon
the biddings made by others or decreasing on sums named by the
auctioneer or person acting as auctioneer, or other person at such sale
or by any other mode of sale by competition,

shall be an auctioneer within the meaning of this Act.
Licence necessary.

### 3.

No person shall, except as hereinafter provided, carry on the business of an
auctioneer unless he or she has obtained from the Comptroller of Inland Revenue a
licence in the form set out in the First Schedule:
Provided that no licence shall be issued to any person until he or she shall give
to the satisfaction of the Comptroller of Inland Revenue good and sufficient security
in the sum of nine hundred and sixty dollars for the faithful performance of the duties
of his or her calling or business and for the purpose of securing the payment of any
fines, penalties, judgments, damages or costs which may be imposed upon or
recovered against him or her as auctioneer.
Duration and conditions of licence.

### 4.

Every such licence shall continue in force from the day on which it is granted
until the 31st of December next ensuing, and shall be subject to such conditions as
are therein specified.
Licence duty.

### 5.

(1) The amount payable to the Comptroller of Inland Revenue for every such
licence shall be fifteen dollars.
(2) Every such amount shall form part of the general revenue of the State.

Refusal and cancellation of licences.

### 6.

(1) Notwithstanding anything contained in the preceding sections, the
Comptroller of Inland Revenue may refuse any application for an auctioneer’s licence
or to renew any such licence and may cancel the licence of any auctioneer who has—
(a) been convicted of any offence under this Act;
(b) failed to account for or pay over any moneys or property which have
come into his or her possession or control as such auctioneer;
(c) parted with any property entrusted to him or her for sale without
obtaining payment for the same and has failed to make good to his or
her employer the value thereof;
(d) otherwise misconducted himself or herself as an auctioneer.
(2) A refusal to issue or to renew, or the cancellation of, an auctioneer’s
licence shall be notified to the person affected thereby under the hand of the
Comptroller of Inland Revenue.
(3) Any person aggrieved by any action of the Comptroller of Inland Revenue
under this section may appeal to the Minister whose decision shall be final.
(Amended by Act 6 of 1976)

List of Licensed Auctioneers to be published.

### 7.

The Comptroller of Inland Revenue shall, at the beginning of every month,
cause to be published in the Gazette and in one or more of the local newspapers, if
any, a list of the persons to whom licences shall have been granted by him or her
during the preceding month; and if at any time he or she shall cancel any such licence
he or she shall publish in like manner as aforesaid the name of the person whose
licence shall have been so cancelled.
Penalty for selling without Licence.

### 8.

Any person who carries on the business of an auctioneer without being duly
licensed shall, on summary conviction, be liable—
(a) for a first offence, to a fine not exceeding six hundred dollars; and
(b) for a second or subsequent offence, to a fine not exceeding three
thousand dollars.
(Amended by Acts 7 of 1976 and 9 of 1986)

Keeping of accounts by auctioneer.

### 9.

(1) Every auctioneer shall keep a regular and correct account of all property
sold by him or her at auction, such account to be written in a book to be kept for that
purpose, in the form contained in the Second Schedule, and every such account shall
be opened to the inspection of every person interested:
Provided that it shall be a sufficient compliance with the provisions of this
subsection if an entry is made in such book of the name of the person to whom the
property sold belongs, together with a reference to some other book where is kept an
account containing the particulars mentioned in the form aforesaid.

(2) Any person who fails to keep such account in the manner hereinbefore
mentioned, or hinders or prevents any person interested from inspecting any such
book, shall be liable to a fine of six hundred dollars.
(Amended by Acts 7 of 1976 and 9 of 1986)

Remuneration payable to auctioneers.

### 10.

(1) On a sale or attempted sale at auction of the properties referred to in the
Third Schedule an auctioneer shall be entitled, in respect of such sale or attempted
sale, to demand and to receive the relevant commission or fee specified in that
Schedule.
(2) Any auctioneer who demands, receives or retains any commission or fee in
excess of the commission or fee hereby chargeable shall be liable to a fine of six
hundred dollars.
(Amended by Acts 7 of 1976 and 9 of 1986)

Power to grant permission to sell at auction without licence.

### 11.

Notwithstanding anything in this Act, the Minister may grant permission to
any Government Officer to sell at auction any property on account of the
Government.
(Amended by Act 6 of 1976)

Exceptions.

### 12.

This Act shall not apply to auction sales—
(a) by the Registrar, Provost-Marshal, bailiff or any other officer of a
Court of Justice on legal process in the exercise of any powers
conferred on them by law;
(b) by any police officer or other public servant in any department of the
Government Service where such sales are held under the provisions of
any written law or departmental regulation.
