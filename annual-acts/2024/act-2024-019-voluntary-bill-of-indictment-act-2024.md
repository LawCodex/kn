SAINT CHRISTOPHER AND NEVIS
No. 19 of 2024
AN ACT to provide for the filing of a voluntary bill of indictment by the Director of
Public Prosecutions in certain circumstances.
[Published 20 th September 2024 – Extra-ordinary Gazette No. 56 of 2024]

BE IT ENACTED by the King’s Most Excellent Majesty, by and with the advice and
consent of the National Assembly of Saint Christopher and Nevis and by the authority of
same, as follows:1.

Short Title and Commencement.

This Act may be cited as the Voluntary Bill of Indictment Act, 2024 and shall come into
operation on a day to be fixed by the Minister by an Order published in the Gazette.

### 2.

Interpretation.
In this Act—
“court” means Magistrate Court or High Court of the Federation of St. Christopher
and Nevis;
“Minister” means the Minister for Justice;
“offence of a sexual nature” means an offence under any Act prohibiting sexual
offences including the Offences Against the Person Act, Cap. 4.21, the Criminal
Law Amendment Act, Cap. 4.05 or the offence of child pornography under the
Electronic Crimes Act, Cap. 4.41;
“pre-trial application” includes but is not limited to
(a) applications for special measures;
(b) submission of no case to answer;
(c) applications to exclude evidence of bad character;
(d) applications to exclude evidence of hearsay;

(e) applications with respect to the calling of expert witnesses;
(f) applications for disclosure of evidential material in the possession of the
prosecution;
(g) applications to inspect exhibits that the prosecution intends to rely on;
(h) applications to stay the voluntary indictment;
“voluntary bill” means a voluntary bill of indictment filed by the Director of
Public Prosecutions in accordance with the provisions of this Act;
“vulnerable witness” means a witness, whose ability to give evidence or the quality
of whose evidence, in the opinion of the Director of Public Prosecutions, is
likely to be affected by reason of—
(a) age or maturity;
(b) physical, intellectual, or psychiatric disability;
(c) trauma suffered by the witness;
(d) the witness’s fear of intimidation;
(e) the linguistic or cultural background of the witness;
(f) the nature of the proceedings;
(g) the nature of the evidence that the witness is expected to give;
(h) the relationship of the witness to any party to the proceeding;
(i) the absence of the witness from the Federation of St. Christopher and
Nevis; or
(j) any other ground of a similar nature;
“witness deemed to be vulnerable” means
(a) a person under the age of 16 years who is a complainant in a matter
involving a sexual offence;
(b) a person who is a complainant in an offence against the person or property
involving domestic violence as defined by the Domestic Violence Act,
Cap. 12.04; and
(c) a complainant in a matter under the Trafficking in Persons (Prevention)
Act, Cap. 4.40.

### 3.

Application of this Act.

(1) Notwithstanding any rule of practice or anything to the contrary in this or any
other written law, the Director of Public Prosecutions may file a voluntary bill of indictment
in the High Court against a person who is charged before a Magistrate’s Court with an
indictable offence whether before or after the coming into operation of this section, whether
or not a preliminary inquiry or committal proceedings have been conducted or pending, in
the manner provided in this section:

(a) where a co-accused is arrested before the date fixed for the trial of another coaccused who has already been indicted and it is desired to join them both in
the same indictment;
(b) where a Magistrate has heard evidence and the depositions taken before him
or her disclose a prima facie case and he or she is unable to complete the
preliminary enquiry because of his:
(i) physical or mental infirmity;
(ii) resignation;
(iii) retirement; or
(iv) death;
(c) where a person is charged with serious or complex fraud;
(d) to deal with
(i) indictable offences under the Anti-Terrorism Act, Cap. 4.02, Trafficking in
Persons (Prevention) Act, Cap. 4.40, the Drugs (Prevention and Abatement
of the Misuse and Abuse of Drugs) Act, Cap. 9.08, the Anti-Corruption
Act, 2023, the Organised Crime (Prevention and Control) Act, Cap. 4.22,
the Whistleblowers Protection Act 2023, the Unauthorised Disclosure of
Official Information Act, 2023, the Proceeds of Crime Act, Cap. 4.28 and
the Firearms Act, Cap. 19.05.
(ii) indictable offences of a violent or sexual nature;
(iii) indictable offences where there is a child witness or complainant;
(iv) indictable offences against a person involving domestic violence as
defined by the Domestic Violence Act, Cap. 12.04;
(v) indictable offences involving a witness who has been assessed as one
subject or potentially could be subject to threats, unlawful interference or
intimidation;
(vi) indictable offences where the constitutional right of the accused person
to a fair trial within a reasonable time is being or is likely to be infringed;
(vii) any other indictable offence involving the evidence of a vulnerable witness
or deemed vulnerable witness; or
(e) where an accused person charged with any indictable offence has indicated
during the course of criminal proceedings in relation to that offence that he or
she intends to plead guilty; or
(f) where the accused person has been remanded pursuant to an order of the
Court on the basis that there is a risk that the person accused of any indictable
offence may flee the jurisdiction of the Federation of St. Christopher and Nevis.
(2) The Director of Public Prosecutions may file a voluntary bill of indictment pursuant
to this Act notwithstanding that it may contain additional indictable offences outside of
subsections (1)(c) and (1)(d) of this section.

### 4.

Documents to be filed with a voluntary bill.

Every voluntary bill shall be signed by the Director of Public Prosecutions or on his or
her behalf by any legal practitioner acting on his or her instructions, and shall be filed with
the Registrar of the High Court, together with—
(a) statements of the evidence of witnesses whom it is proposed to call in support
of the charge;
(b) copies of documentary exhibits to be tendered in evidence in support of the
charge;
(c) a list of exhibits that the prosecution intends to rely on in support of the
charge; and
(d) such additional copies of the voluntary bill and the respective statements
mentioned in paragraphs (a), (b) and (c) as are necessary for service upon the
accused person.

### 5.

Registrar to issue summons for the attendance of the Accused.
Upon the filing of a voluntary bill, the Registrar shall
(a) issue a summons requiring the attendance of the accused person before a
judge at a date specified in the summons, which date shall not be earlier than
seven days after service upon the accused person of the documents mentioned
in section 4; or
(b) if the accused person is in custody, issue an order to the Commissioner of
Corrections to bring him or her before a judge on a specific date,
and the Registrar shall notify the Director of Public Prosecutions accordingly.

### 6.

Filing of voluntary bill stays committal proceedings or summary trial for the same
matter.

(1) Where a voluntary bill is filed against a person who in relation to the subject matter
of the voluntary bill is before a magistrate’s court
(a) charged with a summary offence, or
(b) for committal proceedings the prosecutor shall, within a reasonable time after
the filing of the voluntary bill, produce to the magistrate and to the person
charged, respectively, a copy of the voluntary bill and of the relevant summons
issued by the Registrar under section 5.
(2) Where a voluntary bill and summons have been produced to a magistrate pursuant
to subsection (1), the magistrate may, where it is within his or her jurisdiction to do so, admit
the person charged under the voluntary bill of indictment, to bail conditioned to appear
before the High Court on the relevant date specified in the summons or remand him or her
into custody so to appear; and, upon so admitting the person charged to bail or remanding
him or her into custody, the jurisdiction of the magistrate to deal with him or her in respect
of the charge shall cease, but the warrant of the magistrate shall be sufficient authority for
the detention of the person named therein, by the Commissioner of Corrections.

### 7.

Warrant of arrest may be issued for non-attendance of accused person.

Where the accused person fails to attend upon the date specified in the summons
issued under section 5, or the judge is satisfied that he or she is avoiding service of the
voluntary bill, the attendance of the accused person may be enforced by the issue of a
warrant for his or her arrest.

### 8.

Accused person to plead to voluntary bill at High Court.

(1) Upon the appearance before the judge of an accused person against whom a
voluntary bill is filed, the voluntary bill shall be read over to him or her by the clerk of court
and the accused person shall be required to plead instantly thereto, unless
(a) he or she shall object that copies of the documents mentioned in section 4
have not previously been served upon him or her; or
(b) having been served with copies of the documents as aforesaid, he or she
indicates to the court that he or she wishes to make a submission of no case
to answer.
(2) If upon arraignment the accused person pleads guilty he or she may be convicted
thereon.
(3) The Court may consider the issue of bail as to whether the accused person should
(a) be released on bail pending trial;
(b) be remanded in custody pending trial.
(4) Where a not guilty plea is entered, unless exceptional circumstances apply, the
following procedure should be followed:
(a) a provisional trial date should be set no more than 8 weeks from the date of
plea;
(b) the Court must list a case management hearing on a date deemed appropriate
but not later than 14 days prior to the trial date;
(c) the Court, together with the parties, must manage the case as fully as possible
and timetabling agreed;
(d) the Court must deal with any pre-trial applications at the first case management
wherever possible;
(e) where the accused person wishes to make a pre-trial application, he or she
must give at least 7 calendar days’ notice of same to the Office of the Director
of Public Prosecutions;
(f) where the defendant does not give the 7 calendar days’ notice as aforesaid,
the prosecution is entitled to seek an adjournment to a date that the Court
deems fit in the circumstances.

### 9.

Witness statements deemed to be deposition.

Every statement purporting to be evidence of witnesses submitted under section (4)
shall be deemed a deposition for the purposes of the Evidence Act, Cap. 3.12,

the Larceny Act Cap. 4.16, the Perjury Act, Cap. 4.23, Child Justice Act, Cap. 4.15,
Eastern Caribbean Supreme Court (Saint Christopher and Nevis) Act, Cap. 3.11 and the
Criminal Procedure Act, Cap. 4.06.

### 10.

Judicial Review of a decision to file a voluntary bill of indictment.
(1) A decision of the Director of Public Prosecutions to file a voluntary bill of indictment
under this Act may be subject to judicial review only on the following grounds:
(a) the decision was in excess of the statutory powers of the Director of Public
Prosecutions under this Act;
(b) where, contrary to the provisions of the Constitution, the Director of Public
Prosecutions could be shown to have acted under the direction or control of
another person or authority and to have failed to exercise his or her
independent discretion;
(c) the decision was made in bad faith;
(d) the decision amounts to an abuse of the process of the court;
(e) where the Director of Public Prosecutions has fettered his or her discretion by
a rigid policy.
(2) The Court shall not grant leave in an application for judicial review under this Act
unless it is satisfied that there is an arguable ground for judicial review having a realistic
prospect of success.

### 11.

Where an Accused person indicates his or her intention to plead guilty at Magistrate
Court.
(1) Where an accused person charged with an indictable offence indicates at any
appearance before the Magistrate Court in relation to that offence that he or she intends to
plead guilty, subject to subsection (2), the Magistrate shall(a) inquire of the accused person as to his or her reasons for his or her decision to
plead guilty;
(b) inquire as to whether his or her decision has been made voluntarily,
unequivocally and without improper pressure;
(c) make a record of such questions as have been put to the accused and such
responses as have been made by the accused to such questions;
(d) read over the record of such questions and answers and ask the accused to
sign such record after satisfying himself or herself that the answers are true
and correct;
(e) countersign the record referred to in subsection (1)(d) above.
(2) A Magistrate in subsection (1) may not proceed with the inquiry unless he or she
is satisfied that
(a) the accused person has obtained legal advice; or

(b) where the accused has not obtained legal advice, he or she has waived his or
her right to legal advice in accordance with his or her constitutional right to do
so pursuant to section 10(2)(d) of the Constitution of Saint Christopher and
Nevis.
(3) Where having conducted an enquiry under subsection (1) the Magistrate is satisfied
that the accused person has made his or her decision to plead guilty voluntarily,
unequivocally and without improper pressure, the Magistrate shall submit the record of the
inquiry to the Director of Public Prosecutions for his or her determination as to whether he or
she will file a voluntary bill of indictment.
(4) Where, pursuant to subsection (3), the Director of Public Prosecutions in writing
indicates to the Magistrate that he or she intends to file a voluntary bill of indictment, the
jurisdiction of the Magistrate to deal with him or her in respect of the charge shall cease, but
the warrant of the Magistrate shall be sufficient authority for the detention of the person
named therein, by the Commissioner of Corrections.
(5) Where the Director of Public Prosecutions indicates his or her intention to the
Magistrate to file a voluntary bill of indictment, he or she shall as soon as practicable after
receiving the record of the inquiry in subsection (3), file such indictment.
