SAINT CHRISTOPHER AND NEVIS
No. 12 of 2020
AN ACT to facilitate the provision of advance information relating to cargo and
passengers of a conveyance; to share the information with other States with a view to
identifying the cargo and persons that may pose risks to security while facilitating trade
through electronic data interchange and for matters connected therewith.
[Published 21st September 2020, Extra-Ordinary Gazette No. 72 of 2020.]
BE IT ENACTED by the Queen's Most Excellent Majesty, by and with the advice and
consent of the National Assembly of Saint Christopher and Nevis and by the authority of the
same as follows:

---

### 1.

Short Title and Commencement.

This Act may be cited as the Advance Cargo Information Act, 2020, and shall come into
force on such date as the Minister may, by Order, appoint.

### 2.

Interpretation.
In this Act
“Advance Cargo Information (ACI)” means manifest, airway bill and bill of lading
information or data set out in the First Schedule in respect of
(a) The cargo and stores including all imports, exports in transit and transhipment
of goods,
(b) Any package of which there is no bill of lading or airway bill, on board a
conveyance;
“ACI hit” means a cargo manifest or airway bill and bill of lading or any documentation
that has been flagged by the ACIS;
“Agent” means a person authorised in writing to transact business under this Act on
behalf of another person;
“Agent of the master” means a person authorised in writing to transact business under
this Act on behalf of the master;

“Advance Passenger Information System (APIS)” means the automated electronic data
interchange of API and the screening of API by the competent authority, IMPACS,
against a Watch List for any API hit;
“Advance Passenger Information (API)” means the information or data concerning a
crew member, passenger or any other person travelling in a conveyance;
“Data Message” means a messaging standard for the electronic communication between
cargo stakeholders such as masters, shippers, freight forwarders, ground-handling
agents, and regulators, as well as customs and security agencies;
“Break bulk” means cargo that must be loaded individually, and not in intermodal
containers;
“Bulk” means cargo that is transported unpackaged in large quantities;
“Carrier” means the person transporting goods or in charge of or responsible for the
operation of the means of transport;
“Cargo” means goods, other than mail, stores, crew member’s effects and passenger’s
accompanied baggage, carried on board a conveyance for consideration;
“Cargo information” means statements or facts required under this Act relating to a
consignment of cargo;
“Cargo Manifest” means a list of cargo being transported or warehoused;
“Cargo reporter” includes a freight forwarder, consolidator, and non-vessel operating
common carrier;
“Comptroller” means the Comptroller of Customs or the person for the time being charged
with the administration of this Act;
“Containerized” means packaged freight in uniform, sealed containers for shipment;
“Consolidator” means a person or firm who combines cargo from multiple shippers into
a single shipment, providing consolidation services;
“conveyance” includes a vehicle, aircraft, vessel or other machine used or capable of
being used for the carriage or transportation of persons or goods by land, air or
water or over or above water;
“crew member” means any person employed on board any conveyance during a voyage
or flight, whether or not such person is on any crew list;
“Documentation” means
(a) any paper or other material on which there is writing;
(b) any paper or other material on which there are marks, figures, symbols or
perforations having a meaning for persons qualified to interpret them;
(c) any paper or other material on which a photographic image or any other image
is recorded;

(d) any article or material from which sounds, images or writing is capable of being
produced with or without the aid of a computer or of some other device; or
(e) any information in digital or electronic format;
“Export” means
(a) to take goods or cause goods to be taken outside the territorial sea or contiguous
zone of Saint Christopher and Nevis, or
(b) to load goods, or cause goods to be loaded on a conveyance for use as stores;
“Freight Forwarders” means a person specialising in arranging storage and shipping of
merchandise on behalf of its shippers;
“IMPACS” means the Implementing Agency for Crime and Security established under
the 2006 Agreement establishing the CARICOM Implementing Agency for Crime
and Security;
“import” means to bring goods or cause goods to be brought into Saint Christopher and
Nevis or its territorial waters or contiguous zone;
“Long Haul Flight” means a flight with a duration of more than eight (8) hours;
“master” in relation to a conveyance means the person having or taking charge or
command of a conveyance, and includes an agent of the master and any person
responsible for the operation of the conveyance;
“Minister” means the Minister responsible for Customs;
“Partial Shipment” or “Part Shipment” means the delivery of a shipment of cargo in two
or more consignments;
“Passenger” means a person not being a bona fide member of the crew, travelling or
seeking to travel on a conveyance;
“Passenger manifest” means a list containing the names of persons being transported
into or out of Saint Christopher and Nevis;
“Proper Officer” means a customs officer assigned or designated by the Comptroller in
relation to the discharge of any function or duty under this Act;
“Regional Space” means the collective territories of the Parties to the ACIS which
comprise the Caribbean Community (CARICOM);
“Representative” means a person who is authorised in writing by an importer, exporter,
freight forwarder or master to transact business under this Act;
“Short Haul Flight” means a flight with a duration of less than eight (8) hours;
“transmission” means the process of sending and sharing information using an analogue
or digital information signal;
“Watch list” means a list that is used to watch or track the current activity or movements
of criminals and suspicious or prohibited cargo – including terrorists, other persons
and cargo of interest to the Caribbean Community (CARICOM).

### 3.

Duty to Provide Information.

(1) The master, agent of the master or every cargo reporter of a conveyance whether
laden or in ballast, shall, prior to arrival of the conveyance in Saint Christopher and Nevis,
provide to the Proper Officer through the ACIS cargo and passenger information set out in
the First and Second Schedules.
(2) Where the distance from Saint Christopher and Nevis to a port of departure of a
conveyance arriving in Saint Christopher and Nevis or for any reason approved by the
Comptroller does not permit the master to provide the information within the time specified in
the Third Schedule, the Comptroller
(a) may exempt the master of the conveyance from the requirements set out in that
Schedule; and
(b) shall establish alternative notification arrangements for the conveyance to
which this subsection applies.
(3) The master or agent of the master and every cargo reporter shall, prior to the
departure of a conveyance, provide to the Proper Officer through the ACIS, the cargo and
passenger information set out in the First and Second Schedules.
(4) The master of an aircraft or vessel that makes a technical stop shall not be required
to provide advance information under section 3 if the arrival is compelled by any emergency,
accident, unfavourable weather conditions, or other necessity and the master of an aircraft
or vessel that makes a technical stop shall
(a) immediately report to the Comptroller;
(b) only permit the cargo to be off loaded from the aircraft or vessel if consent is
given by the Comptroller;
(c) comply with any directions given by the Comptroller in respect of any cargo
on board the aircraft or vessel.
(5) The Comptroller may waive the requirement for a master to provide advance
information as required under section 3 in such circumstances and subject to such conditions
as the Comptroller may prescribe, if the conveyance is
(a) a military or law enforcement aircraft or vessel; or
(b) on official State business.
4 Penalty.
A master or cargo reporter who fails to comply with the requirements of or arrangements
established under this Act, commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine
of thirty thousand dollars or to a term of imprisonment not exceeding three years.

Use of advance information.

(1) Subject to the Data Protection Act, 2018, the Electronic Crimes Act, 2009 and any
other relevant law, the Comptroller may disclose or authorise the disclosure of any document

or information in accordance with any law, treaty, agreement or arrangement concluded by
Saint Christopher and Nevis.
(2) The information which may be disclosed under subsection (7) includes, but is not
limited to, information about
(a) the movement of a vessel or an aircraft, including passenger and crew lists;
(b) past travel movements, general history and modus operandi of specified persons;
(c) currency and documents relating to other relevant financial transactions,
including money laundering;
(d) intelligence analysis, assessments and reports; and
(e) the details about known or suspected involvement of persons in illicit activities.
(3) IMPACS shall use the cargo and passenger data to conduct screening against
databases and Watch lists of cargo, crew members and passengers on conveyances that
enter into, depart from and move within the Regional Space.
(4) ACI and passenger data shall only be used for the purposes of this Act.
6 Retention of advance information.
Advanced cargo information collected under this Act shall be retained by the ACIS for
a period not exceeding seven (7) years from the date of collection of the information, and
advanced passenger information collected under this Act shall be retained in accordance
with the provisions of the Advanced Passenger Information Act 2019.
7 Powers of the Minister.
(1) The Minister may, by Order subject to negative resolution, amend the First and
Third Schedules.
(2) The Minister may make Regulations that may prescribe procedures for the reporting
and processing of advance cargo information.

## THIRD SCHEDULE

(Section 3)
DATA SUBMISSION TIMEFRAME

1\.

The information must be submitted in keeping CARICOM’s messaging standards
specifications as set out in Part 1 of the First Schedule

2\.

The information set out in Part 2 of the First Schedule (Advance Cargo
Information) shall be provided at the latest:
(a) For incoming cargo (i.e. import) by aviation
i.

in the case of short haul, at time of “Wheels Up” of aircraft except where
the duration of the flight is less than one (1) hour the ACI shall be provided
one (1) hour before arrival at the first port in the country of destination.

ii.

in the case of long haul, 4 hours prior to arrival at the first port in the
country of destination.

(b) For outgoing cargo (short and long haul), at the time of departure (Wheels
Up”) of the aircraft.
(c) For incoming cargo (i.e. import) by maritime
i.

Containerized cargo: 24 hours before arrival; at first port in the country
of destination

ii.

Bulk/Break bulk: 24 hours before arrival at first port in the country of
destination.

(d) For outgoing cargo (i.e. export) by maritime
I.

Containerized cargo: 24 hours before loading at port of departure;

II.

Bulk/Break bulk: 24 hours before departure to the first port in the country
of destination.

(e) For incoming cargo (i.e. imported) by Road:
I.

1 hour prior to arrival at the first port in the country of destination.

(f) For outgoing cargo (i.e. exported) by Road:
II. 1 hour prior to departure to the first port in the country of destination.
3\.

The information set out in the Second Schedule (Advance Passenger Information)
shall be provided:
(a) in the case of commercial or private aircraft, not later than 40 minutes prior to
departure from their last port of call;
(b) in the case of a vessel or conveyance arriving from a member state of the
Caribbean Community, not later than one hour prior to its arrival from that
