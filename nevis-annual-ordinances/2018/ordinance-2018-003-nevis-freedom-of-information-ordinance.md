## PART I- PRELIMINARY

### 1.

Short title.
This Ordinance may be cited as the Nevis Freedom of Information Ordinance.

### 2.

Interpretation.
(1) In this Ordinance, unless the context otherwise requires:
“applicant” means a person who has made a request under section 26 or a person
who makes a request for amendment of a record under section 39, as the case
may be;
“Commissioner” means the Commissioner of Information appointed under
section 6;
“Department” means a Department of the Nevis Island Administration.
“document” means information recorded in any form, whether printed or on tape or
film or by electronic means or otherwise and includes public contracts, grants
or leases of land, letters, e-mails, written decisions, policy papers, maps,
diagrams, plans, photographs, films or micro films, and any article or thing that
has been so treated in relation to any sounds or visual images that those
sounds or visual images are capable, with or without the aid of some other
device, of being reproduced from the article or thing, and includes a copy of
any such information, but does not include library material maintained for
reference purposes;

“exempt document” means a document that is exempt from disclosure under this
Ordinance by virtue of a provision of Part V;
“exempt matter” means any matter the inclusion of which in a document causes the
document to be an exempt document;
“exempt record” have the same meaning as assigned to the term “exempt document”;
“Head” in relation to a public authority listed in column 1 of the Schedule means
that person listed in column 2 of the Schedule opposite that public authority;
“information officer” means a senior officer of a Ministry or the designated person
appointed pursuant to section 77.
“Minister” means the Minister of Government to whom is assigned responsibility
for Information and Information Services;
“Ministry” means a Ministry of the Nevis Island Administration;
“NIA” means the Nevis Island Administration
“personal information” means information recorded in any form about an identifiable
individual, including(a) information relating to the race, national or ethnic origin, religion, age, sex
or marital status of the individual;
(b) information relating to the education or the medical, psychiatric,
psychological, criminal or employment history of the individual or
information relating to financial transactions in which the individual has
been involved;
(c) any identifying number, symbol or other particular assigned to the
individual;
(d) the address, telephone number, fingerprints or blood type of the individual;
(e) the name of the individual where it appears with other personal information
relating to the individual or where the disclosure of the name itself would
reveal information about the individual;
(f) correspondence sent to a public authority by the individual that is explicitly
or implicitly of a private or confidential nature, and replies to such
correspondence that would reveal the contents of the original
correspondence; or
(g) the views or opinions of any other person about the individual.
“prescribed” means prescribed by regulations made under section 73;
“public authority” means an entity listed in column 1 of the Schedule and includes:
(a) a local authority;
(b) a public statutory corporation or body; or

(c) a body corporate or an unincorporated body established for a public
purpose, which may be prescribed by the Minister by Order published in
the Gazette;
“principal officer” means–
(a) in relation to a Ministry, the Permanent Secretary of that Ministry,
(b) in relation to a Department, the Head of that Department, and
(c) in relation to a public authority, the chief executive officer, general manager
or other similar officer of that authority;
“record” shall have the same meaning as assigned to the term “document”;
“regulations” means regulations made under this Ordinance;
“request” means a request for access to a document or to amend record of personal
information made in accordance with this Ordinance.
“requester” shall have the same meaning as assigned to the term “applicant”;
“third party”, in relation to a request to a public authority for access to a record
means any person (other than the requester or the public authority) who gave
information in the record to a public authority in confidence, or any person to
whom information in the record relates;
“working days” means a number of days, excluding Saturday, Sunday or a public
holiday.
(2) In this Ordinance, a reference to a document includes, where the context requires, a
part of a document.
(3) In this Ordinance, a reference to a document that is held by a public authority
includes a document that is in the possession or custody of, or is under the control of, that
authority.
(4) For the purposes of this Ordinance, any document that is held by an independent
contractor relating to a contract for which the contractor is engaged by a public authority is
deemed to be a document held by that public authority; and there is deemed to be included
in the contract a provision that the contractor shall, if requested by the public authority
concerned, give a copy of the document to the public authority.
(5) Nothing in this Ordinance precludes a person from obtaining access to records
under any other provision of law.

### 3.

Objects of the Ordinance.
(1) The objects of this Ordinance are to:
(a) give the public the right to obtain access to information held by public
authorities, subject only to exceptions that are in the public interest or for the
protection of the rights of persons in respect of whom information is collected
and held by public authorities.

(b) increase transparency and accountability of public authorities, and eliminate
unnecessary secrecy, with regard to information held by public authorities;
(c) inform the public about the activities of public authorities, including the manner
in which decisions are made by them that would affect members of the public;
(d) standardize the placement of information concerning public authorities in the
public domain as a matter of routine; and
(e) establish the Office of the Commissioner of Information and specify the
functions of that office.
(2) This Ordinance must receive such purposive and liberal construction and
interpretation as will best ensure the attainment of its objects.
(3) Any discretion conferred by this Ordinance shall be exercised as far as possible so
as to facilitate and promote, promptly and at the lowest reasonable cost, the disclosure of
information in accordance with this Ordinance.

### 4.

Application.
(1) Subject to subsection (2), this Act does not apply to:
(a) records relating to the exercise of judicial or quasi-judicial functions by any
court, tribunal or other body or person; or
(b) records relating to the Justice Protection Programme or Investigative Agency,
held by the Saint Christopher and Nevis Police Force in accordance with the
Justice Protection Act 2012;
(c) records obtained or created by any of the following public authorities in the
course of carrying out their functions:
(i) the Department of Audit
(ii) the Office of the Commissioner of Information;
(iii) the Legal Department;
(iv) the Department of Internal Audit.
(d) a Commission of Inquiry and the members and staff of a Commission of Inquiry;
(e) the internal proceedings of NIA which are subject to parliamentary privilege.

(2) The reference to records in subsection (1) does not include records relating to the
general administration of:
(a) any court, tribunal or other body or person referred to in subsection (1)(a); or
(b) any public authority referred to in subsection (1)(c).

## PART II- ADMINISTRATION

### 5.

Powers and duties of the Minister.

The Minister must ensure consistency and continuity in the administration of this
Ordinance in accordance with the objects set out in section 3.

### 6.

Establishment and appointment of Commissioner of Information.
(1) The Office of Commissioner of Information is established as a public office.

(2) The Commissioner of Information shall be appointed by the Governor General after
consultation with the Premier, who shall first have consulted the Leader of the Opposition.
(3) A person shall not be qualified to hold office as Commissioner if that person(a) is a member of the House of Assembly;
(b) holds or is acting in a public office;
(c) is an undischarged bankrupt, having been declared bankrupt under any law;
(d) has been convicted of theft, fraud or other such offence involving dishonesty;
or
(e) holds a political office or is an employee of, a political party or is a political
activist.
(4) The Commissioner shall be appointed for a period of five years and may be
reappointed for a further period of five years, but shall not hold office for more than two (2)
consecutive terms.
(5) In the exercise of his functions, the Commissioner shall not be subject to the direction
or control of any other person or authority.
(6) The Commissioner shall not hold any office of profit other than that of Commissioner
or otherwise engage in any occupation for reward outside the duties of Commissioner of
Information unless authorised in writing by the Governor General on recommendation of the
Premier.
(7) The Commissioner may be removed from office by the Governor-General for inability
to exercise the functions of his office (as a result of infirmity of body or mind or any other
cause) or for misbehaviour, on the recommendation of the Premier after consultation with the
Leader of the Opposition.

### 7.

Mandate of Commissioner.

(1) The mandate of the Commissioner is to promote public access to information in
accordance with this Ordinance, including by raising public awareness and understanding
of the rights herein conferred and by providing guidance to public authorities with regard to
the obligations imposed on them by the Ordinance.
(2) The Commissioner shall perform the functions assigned to him by this Ordinance
and by any other statutory provision.

### 8.

Functions of the Commissioner.

In addition to any other functions under this Ordinance, the functions of the Commissioner
shall be:
(a) to publicise the requirements of this Ordinance and the rights and obligations
arising hereunder;

(b) to monitor and report to NIA on the compliance by Ministries and prescribed
authorities with their obligations;
(c) to make recommendations for reforms of a general or specific nature to facilitate
compliance with this Ordinance;
(d) to undertake, facilitate or promote the training of officials of Ministries and
prescribed authorities and other persons on the right to information and the
effective implementation of this Ordinance;

### 9.

Powers of Commissioner.
(1) In conducting a review, the Commissioner has power(a) to summon and enforce the appearance of persons and compel them to give
oral or written evidence on oath or affirmation, and to produce such documents
and things as the Commissioner deems requisite to conduct the review, in the
same manner and to the same extent as a judge of the Supreme Court;
(b) to administer oaths and affirmations;
(c) to receive and accept such evidence and other information, whether on oath or
by affidavit or otherwise, as the Commissioner sees fit, whether or not the
evidence or information is or would be admissible in a court of law;
(d) to enter any premises occupied by any public authority on complying with any
security requirements of the authority relating to the premises;
(e) to converse in private with any person in any premises entered pursuant to
paragraph (d) and otherwise carry out therein such inquiries authorised under
this Ordinance as the Commissioner sees fit; and
(f) to examine or obtain copies of or extracts from books or other records found in
any premises entered pursuant to paragraph (d) containing any matter relevant
to the investigation.

(2) Notwithstanding any Act or other Ordinance or any privilege under the law of
evidence, the Commissioner may, during the conduct of a review under this Ordinance,
examine any record to which this Ordinance applies that is under the control of a public
authority, and no such record may be withheld from the Commissioner on any grounds.
(3) The Commissioner may return any document or thing produced to him by any
person or public authority under this section within ten (10) days of a request by that person
or public authority for its return but nothing in this subsection precludes the Commissioner
from making a subsequent request for its production in accordance with this section.
(4) A person who fails or refuses to comply with a requirement under this section or
who hinders or obstructs the Commissioner in the performance of his functions under this
section is guilty of an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding
$5,000 or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 6 months or both.

### 10.

Review and investigations by Commissioner.
(1) The Commissioner shall keep the operation of this Ordinance under review and
shall, not later than two years after this section comes into operation, carry out an investigation
into practices and procedures adopted by public authorities generally for the purposes of
compliance with the provisions of this Ordinance.
(2) The Commissioner shall prepare a written report on the investigation under
subsection (1) and provide a copy of the report to the Minister.
(3) The Commissioner may at any time carry out other investigations into the practices
and procedures adopted by public authorities generally or any particular public authority for
the purposes of compliance with the provisions of this Ordinance.
(4) The Commissioner may prepare a report of his findings and conclusions resulting
from the carrying out of an investigation under subsection (3).
(5) The Commissioner shall cause a copy of any report under this section:
(a) to be provided to the Minister and to any public authority concerned and;
(b) to be appended to the Commissioner’s annual report under section 11.

### 11.

Annual report by Commissioner.
(1) The Commissioner shall, within the first three months of each calendar year, prepare
a report on the operation of this Ordinance during the previous calendar year and cause
copies of the report to be submitted to the Minister.
(2) The report shall include the following information:
(a) the number of requests made under section 26 for access to records and how
they were disposed of;
(b) the number of decisions that an applicant was not entitled to access to a
document pursuant to a request, the provisions of this Ordinance under which
these decisions were made and the number of times each provision was invoked;
(c) the number of requests made under section 39 to amend records of personal
information and how they were disposed of;
(d) the number of applications made for internal review under Part VI and how they
were disposed of;
(e) the number of applications for review by the Commissioner under part VII and
how they were disposed of; and
(f) the number of applications made under section 72 for judicial review and the
status or result of such applications.
(g) the amount of charges collected by each public authority under this Ordinance;
(h) any other facts which indicate an effort by public authorities to administer and
implement the spirit and intention of this Ordinance.

(3) The Minister shall cause a copy of each annual report submitted pursuant to
subsection (1) to be laid before NIA as soon as practicable after he has received the report.
(4) Every public authority shall, before the end of each calendar year, provide a written
report to the Commissioner containing the information set out in subsection (2) as it pertains
to that public authority.
(5) The Commissioner shall cause proper accounts to be kept and maintained of all the
financial transactions with respect to the Office of Commissioner and shall prepare in respect
of each financial year, a statement of such accounts in such form as the Accountant General
may direct.

### 12.

Staff of the Office of the Commissioner of Information.
(1) There shall be appointed, with the concurrence of the Commissioner and in
accordance with the laws governing appointments to the public service, officers and employees
to assist the Commissioner in the performance of the functions of the Commissioner.
(2) The Commissioner may, in addition, engage from time to time such technical or
professional advisers as the Commissioner considers necessary to assist in the discharge of
his functions under this Ordinance.
(3) All officers and employees of the office of the Commissioner and any other persons
authorised to perform any functions under this Ordinance shall be under the control and
direction of the Commissioner and shall perform their functions without fear, favour or
prejudice; and no person or authority shall interfere with or exert undue influence on them in
the exercise of their powers or in the performance of their professional functions.

### 13.

Obligation to maintain secrecy.
(1) The Commissioner and every person appointed or engaged under section 12 shall
maintain secrecy in respect of all matters that come to their knowledge in the exercise of their
functions and shall only communicate any such matter to any person for the purpose of
carrying out their functions under this Ordinance.
(2) Information or documents obtained by the Commissioner or any person appointed
or engaged under section 12 in the exercise of their functions shall not be disclosed except in
order to comply with the provisions of this Ordinance.

### 14.

Exemption from personal liability.
(1) The Commissioner, any officer or employee of his Office or any other person acting
on behalf of or under the direction of the Commissioner shall not be personally liable in
criminal or civil proceedings for any act done or omitted to be done in the performance or
intended performance of their functions under this Ordinance in good faith pursuant to this
Ordinance.
(2) Neither the Commissioner nor any person appointed or engaged under section 12
shall be called to give evidence in any court, or in any proceedings of a judicial nature, in
respect of anything coming to their knowledge in the exercise of their functions under this
Ordinance.

## PART III- PROVISION OF INFORMATION BY PUBLIC AUTHORITIES

### 15.

Guide to using the Ordinance.
Every information officer of a Ministry or public authority appointed under section 77
shall, within three months of the commencement of this Ordinance, compile a guide containing
practical information to facilitate the effective exercise of rights pursuant to this Ordinance,
and shall disseminate the guide widely in the most accessible form of disseminating
information utilised by the Ministry or public authority.

### 16.

Information statement.
(1) Every Ministry or public authority shall prepare an information statement setting
out:
(a) a description of the structure and organisation of the authority and the legislation
governing the authority as well as relevant details concerning any services it
provides directly to members of the public
(b) a description of the functions, powers and duties of the authority and that of
its senior officers, including its obligations under this Ordinance;
(c) a summary of the services that the authority provides generally, including
ongoing programs of the authority;
(d) a description of all categories of documents that are held by the authority, in
sufficient detail to facilitate the exercise of the right of access under this
Ordinance;
(e) a description of all manuals used by employees of the authority in administering
or carrying out the programs and activities of the authority;
(f) a description of the policies, rules and guidelines used by the authority to
make decisions or recommendations;
(g) the name and contact information of the person designated by the authority
under section 77 as the person to whom requests under this Ordinance may be
directed;
(h) a statement of the procedure to be followed by a person when a request for
access to a document is made to a public authority;
(i) a statement listing all boards, councils, committees and other bodies that have
been established for the purpose of advising, the public authority, and whose
meetings are open to the public, or the minutes of whose meetings are available
for public inspection; and
(j) any other information that the head of the authority considers relevant for the
purpose of facilitating access by the public to information held by the authority;
(2) Every public authority shall update its information statement at least once a year,
but may do so more frequently.
(3) Every public authority shall cause its information statement, as updated from time
to time, to be made available for inspection by the public at reasonable times by:

(a) keeping copies at its principal office;
(b) providing a copy to the Charlestown Public Library and Gingerland Public
Library; and
(c) making copies available electronically, including on any website of the public
authority.
(4) A public authority shall publish a notice in the Gazette indicating the places where
its information statement is available for inspection by the public.
(5) Every public authority shall provide a copy of its information statement, as updated
from time to time, to the Commissioner.
(6) Nothing in this section requires the publication of information that is of such a
nature that its inclusion in a document would cause that document to be an exempt document.
(7) Where a public authority is created on or after the commencement of this Ordinance,
the public authority shall comply with subsection (1), as soon as practicable after its creation
but in any case at least within one (1) calendar year of its creation.

### 17.

Provision of other information.
(1) A public authority shall provide information to the general public about information
it holds, on an annual basis, by:
(a) print media;
(b) radio media;
(c) television media; or
(d) online web-page,
and on how to access information held by the public authority.
(2) Public authorities shall keep a log of all access requests made under section 26,
excluding the names of the applicants, any other information that could reveal the identity of
the applicant and any personal information about any person.
(3) The log shall indicate whether a request has been granted or refused and, in the
case of a refusal, the log shall include the reasons for refusal.
(4) The information in the log, other than information that has been provided pursuant
to an access request, shall be made available to members of the public upon request.
(5) Public authorities shall make their quarterly expenditures available to members of
the public upon request.
(6) Public authorities shall cause to be published in the Gazette on a regular basis
details of every contract entered into by the authority that has a total value of $50,000 or
more, including the name of the contractor, the monetary value of the contract, the goods
and services to be provided under the contract and the time for performance of the contract
(completion or delivery date).

(7) In January of each year, the Minister shall publish a list setting out the title and
corresponding salary range of every position of public officer within the meaning of
section 3 of the Pensions Act of Saint Christopher and Nevis.
(8) Publication of the list referred to in subsection (7) shall be by posting a copy of the
list on the Nevis Island Administration website and by providing copies for inspection by
the public at the Charlestown Library and Gingerland Library.

### 18.

Powers of Commissioner in relation to information statements.
(1) The Commissioner may:
(a) provide guidance to public authorities with regard to the preparation of
information statements; and
(b) review the information statement of any public authority to determine if it
complies with section 16.
(2) Where a public authority has not published an information statement or where the
Commissioner determines that an authority’s statement does not comply with section 16, the
Commissioner may issue an order to the authority requiring it to publish its statement or to
bring it into compliance with section 16, as the case may be, within a period to be specified by
the Commissioner.

### 19.

Powers of Commissioner in relation to other information.
(1) The Commissioner may provide guidance to public authorities with regard to the
provision of other information under section 17.
(2) Where the Commissioner determines that a public authority has not complied with
section 17 the Commissioner may issue an order to the authority requiring it to comply with
section 17 within a period to be specified by the Commissioner.

### 20.

Effect of order by Commissioner.
An order issued by the Commissioner under section 18 or 19 is binding on the public
authority to which it has been issued and, upon the order being filed with the Registrar of the
Supreme Court, it shall have the effect of an order of the Supreme Court and shall be enforceable
in the same manner as an order of the court.

### 21.

Role of Commissioner generally under this Part.
The Commissioner shall foster and encourage the publication by public authorities of
information of relevance or interest to the general public in relation to the activities and
functions of the authority generally, in addition to the information that is required to be
provided under this Part.

### 22.

Saving for exempt records.
Nothing in this Part requires a public authority to publish or provide any information
that constitutes an exempt record under this Ordinance.

## PART IV-RIGHT OFACCESS TO INFORMATION

### 23.

Right to access information.
(1) Subject to this Ordinance, every person who is a citizen of Saint Christopher and
Nevis or a resident of Saint Christopher and Nevis has a right to and shall, on request, be
given access to any document that is held by a public authority, other than an exempt record.
(2) Public authorities shall make every reasonable effort to:
(a) assist persons in connection with requests; and
(b) respond to requests completely, accurately and in a timely manner.
(3) An applicant is not required to give any reasons for making a request for access to
information held by a public authority.
(4) The identity of an applicant shall be kept confidential and, except with the consent
of the applicant, may not be disclosed to any person other than a person who is required to
deal with the request under this Ordinance.

### 24.

Access to documents apart from this Ordinance.
Nothing in this Ordinance is intended to prevent or discourage Ministries and prescribed
authorities from publishing or giving access to documents (including exempt documents),
otherwise than as required by this Ordinance, where they can properly do so or are required
by law to do so.

### 25.

Access procedures not to apply to certain documents.
A person is not entitled to obtain, in accordance with the procedure provided for in this
Part, access to:
(a) a document which contains information that is open to public access, as part of
a public register or otherwise, in accordance with another written law, where
that access is subject to a fee or other charge;
(b) a document which contains information that is available for purchase by the
public in accordance with arrangements made by a public authority;
(c) a document that is available for public inspection in a registry maintained by
the Registrar or other public authority;
(d) a document which is stored for preservation or safe custody, being a document
which is a duplicate of a document of a public authority.

### 26.

Request for access to documents.
(1) A request for access to a document under this Ordinance must be made in writing to
the public authority that holds or is reasonably expected to hold the document.
(2) A request must identify the document or contain sufficient information to enable
the designated officer of the public authority, or an employee of the public authority who is
familiar with the relevant documents, to identify the record by taking reasonable steps to do
so.

(3) A request may specify in which form or manner the applicant wishes to be given
access and, subject to subsection 37 (2), access shall be given in that form or manner.
(4) Subject to section 32, a request under this section may be made for access to all
records of a particular description or all records relating to a particular subject.
(5) A public authority shall acknowledge receipt of a request within five (5) working
days after receipt of the request, and the authority shall at the same time inform the applicant
of the process for dealing with the request and of the applicant’s rights under this Ordinance.

### 27.

Duty to assist applicants.
(1) A public authority shall take reasonable steps to assist any person who:
(a) wishes to make a request under section 26; or
(b) has made a request which does not comply with the requirements of section 26
to make a request in a manner which complies with that section.
(2) Where a request in writing is made to a public authority for access to an official
document, the public authority shall not refuse the request on the ground that the request
does not comply with section 26 without first giving the applicant a reasonable opportunity
of consultation with the public authority with a view to amending the request so that it
complies with the form required by section 26.
(3) Without prejudice to section 34, a public authority shall take reasonable steps to
assist any person in the exercise of any other right under this Ordinance.

### 28.

Transfer of requests to access documents.
(1) Where a request under section 26 is received by a public authority and any record
requested is not held by that authority but, to the knowledge of that authority, is held by one
or more other public authorities, the public authority that received the request shall, not later
than ten (10) working days after receipt of the request cause a copy of the request to be
given:
(a) to that other public authority; or
(b) in the case of more than one other public authority, to the authority whose
functions are, in the opinion of the head of the public authority that first
received the request, most closely related to the subject matter of the request.
(2) The head of the public authority that first received the request shall inform the
applicant in writing of the other public authority or authorities to whom a copy of the request
has been given.
(3) A public authority to whom a copy of a request has been given under subsection (1)
shall be deemed to have received the request at the time of the receipt by that authority of the
copy, and section 26, subsections (3), (4) and (5) shall apply in respect of the request that
has been copied to the authority.

### 29.

Time limit for determining request.
(1) A public authority shall take reasonable steps to enable an applicant to be notified
of the approval or refusal of his request as soon as practicable but in any case not later than
thirty working days after the day on which the request is duly made.
(2) Where an applicant is required to amend a request in order for it to meet the
requirements set out in section 26, then for the purposes of computing time limit, the period
commencing on the day an applicant is given notice to amend the request and ending on the
day the applicant confirms or alters the request is to be disregarded in the computation of the
thirty-day period referred to in this section.

### 30.

Giving of access.
(1) Subject to this Ordinance, where:
(a) a request is duly made by an applicant to a public authority for access to an
official document,
(b) the request is approved by the public authority, and
(c) any fee that is required to be paid before access is granted has been paid,
the public authority shall forthwith give the applicant access to the official document.
(2) Where:
(a) a decision is made not to grant a request for access to a document on the
ground that it is an exempt document;
(b) it is practicable for the public authority to grant access to a copy of the document
with such deletions as to make the copy not an exempt document; and
(c) it appears from the request, or the applicant subsequently indicates, that the
applicant would wish to have access to such a copy, the public authority shall
give the applicant access to such a copy of the document.

### 31.

Decision on request.
(1) Subject to the provisions of this Ordinance, a public authority shall, not later than
thirty (30) days after receipt of a request under section 26, decide whether to grant or refuse
to grant the request in whole or in part and shall immediately after a decision has been made
give notice in writing of the decision to the applicant and to any third party who may have
made representations pursuant to section 61.
(2) Where the decision is to deny the request, the notice referred to in subsection (1)
must specify the reasons for the decision including findings on any material issues related to
the decision, any public interest considerations that impacted the decision and the procedure
for review of the decision under the Ordinance.
(3) Where the decision is to grant a request, the public authority concerned shall
decide the form and manner in which the right of access to the relevant record is to be given,
the fee payable for the provision of access and access shall be given in accordance with
section 30 unless an application for an internal review of the decision to grant access has
been made.

(4) Where an application has been made under section 64 for an internal review of a
decision to grant a request where the public authority concerned is minded to provide
access to the record concerned, access shall be granted as soon as possible after:
(a) the date when the period under section 64 for applying for an internal review of
a decision has expired without an application having been made; or
(b) if an application under section 68 has been made, the date when a review under

## Part VII in respect of a decision made under section 64 has been completed.

### 32.

Extension of time.
(1) A public authority may extend the original period of thirty (30) days referred to in
section 31(1) by such further period, not exceeding thirty (30) days, as the authority considers
necessary if, in the opinion of the head of the authority, compliance with the original period
of thirty (30) days is not reasonably practicable because:
(a) there is insufficient time to consider representations made by third parties
under section 61 or to complete any consultations with regard to whether
access to particular information is in the public interest; or
(b) dealing with the request within the original period of thirty (30) days would
substantially or unreasonably interfere with the day to day operations of the
authority.
(2) Where a period is extended by a public authority under this section, the authority
shall, before the expiration of the original period, give notice in writing to the applicant
concerned of the extension and the reasons for it.

### 33.

Deferment of access.
(1) A public authority which receives a request may defer giving access to the document
concerned if the document has been prepared:
(a) for presentation to NIA;
(b) for release to the media; or
(c) solely for inclusion, in the same or in an amended form in a document to be
prepared for a purpose specified in paragraph (a) or (b),
and the document is yet to be presented or released, or included in a document to be
presented or released, as the case may be.
(2) Where the provision of access to a document is deferred in accordance with
subsection (1), the public authority shall inform the applicant of the reasons for the decision
and indicate the approximate period for which the deferment will operate.

### 34.

Refusal of request on administrative grounds.
(1) A public authority may refuse to grant a request if:
(a) the record requested does not exist or cannot be found after reasonable steps
have been taken to find it;

(b) the request does not contain sufficient information to enable the authority to
identify the record by taking reasonable steps and the applicant has failed to
amend the application accordingly;
(c) in the opinion of the head of the authority, granting the request would, by
reason of the number or nature of the records requested, or that the request is
expressed to relate to all documents, or to all documents of a specified class,
that contain information of a specified kind or relate to a specified subject
matter, require the retrieval and examination of such number of records or an
examination of records of such kind as to cause a substantial and unreasonable
interference with or disruption of the other work of the public authority having
regard to any difficulty that would exist in identifying, locating or collating
documents containing the relevant information within the filing system of the
Ministry or prescribed authority;
(d) publication of the record is required by law and is intended to be effected not
later than three months after the receipt of the request by the authority;
(e) the request is, in the opinion of the head of the authority, frivolous or vexatious;
(f) the information requested is already in the public domain, is reasonably
accessible to the public or is reasonably available to the public on request
under any other statutory provision; or
(g) the fee payable under section 41 has not been paid.
(2) A public authority shall not refuse to grant a request under subsection (1)(b) or (c),
unless the authority has assisted, or offered to assist, the applicant to amend the request in
a manner such that it no longer falls under those provisions.

### 35.

Reasons to be given for deferment or refusal.
(1) Where in relation to a request for access to a document of a public authority, a
decision is made under this Part that the applicant is not entitled to access to the document
as requested, that provision of access to the document be deferred or that no such document
exists, the public authority shall cause the applicant to be given notice in writing of the
decision, and the notice shall:
(a) state the findings referring to the material on which those findings were based,
and the reasons for the decision;
(b) where the decision relates to a public authority, state the name and designation
of the person giving the decision;
(c) inform the applicant of his right to apply to the High Court for judicial review of
the decision and the time within which the application for review is required to
be made;
(2) In a notice under subsection (1), a public authority:
(a) shall not be required to include any matter that is of such a nature that its
inclusion in a document of a public authority would cause that document to be
an exempt document;

(b) if the decision relates to a request for access to a document which is an exempt
document or which, if it existed, would be an exempt document may state the
decision in terms which neither confirm nor deny the existence of any document.

### 36.

Manner of access to records.
(1) A public authority shall give access to a record under this Ordinance by providing
the applicant with the information in the record in any of the following forms that it considers
appropriate:
(a) a reasonable opportunity to inspect the document;
(b) a copy of the document;
(c) a transcript of the information;
(d) an electronic machine-readable device or other electronic device that contains
the information;
(e) a reasonable opportunity to hear or view the record, where the record is of
sound or visual images;
(f) a decoded copy of the information, where the information is in shorthand or
another code;
(g) such other means as may be determined by the public authority.
(2) Where a public authority decides to grant a request and the request is for access to
a record in a particular form or manner, access shall be given in that form or manner unless the
authority is satisfied that:
(a) access in another form or manner specified in subsection (1) would be
significantly more efficient; or
(b) the giving of access in the form or manner requested would:
(i) be physically detrimental to the record or would be detrimental to the
preservation of the document or, having regard to the physical nature of
the document, would not be appropriate; or
(ii) would interfere unreasonably with the operations of the Ministry or
prescribed authority; or
(iii) involve an infringement of copyright (other than copyright owned by the
Crown, the Nevis Island Administration or the public authority concerned);
(iv) conflict with a legal duty or obligation of the public authority concerned;
or
(v) affect the protection of an exempt record from disclosure.
(3) Where a public authority decides to grant a request but, for reasons set out in
subsection (2), does not give access in the form or manner specified in the request, the
authority shall give access in such other form as the authority considers appropriate.

### 37.

Access to part of record.
(1) Subject to subsection (2), where a record requested contains information that is
exempt, the public authority concerned shall, if it is practicable, prepare a copy, in such form
as the authority considers appropriate, of so much of the record requested as does not
consist of exempt material, and the request shall be granted by offering the applicant access
to the copy in accordance with section 30 unless a copy of the document would be misleading.
(2) Where an applicant is offered access to a copy of part of a record under this
section, the notice under section 31 shall specify that such access is offered pursuant to this
section and that the copy does not purport to be a copy of the complete record requested.

### 38.

Decision to be made by authorised person.
(1) A decision in respect of a request made to a Ministry or prescribed authority may be
made on behalf of the Ministry or prescribed authority by the responsible Minister or the
principal officer of the Ministry or prescribed authority or, subject to the Regulations, by an
officer of the Ministry or prescribed authority acting within the scope of authority exercisable
by him in accordance with the arrangements approved by the responsible Minister or the
principal officer of the Ministry or prescribed authority.
(2) Where a request is made to a public authority for a document, and no arrangements
in respect of documents of that type have been made and published under the Regulations,
a decision on that request shall, for the purpose of enabling an application for judicial review
to be made, be deemed to have been made by the responsible Minister of the public authority.

### 39.

Request to amend record of personal information.
(1) Where personal information in a record held by a public authority is incomplete,
incorrect or misleading, the authority shall, on request in writing by or on behalf of the
person to whom the information relates, amend the record:
(a) by altering it so as to make the information complete or correct or not misleading,
as the case may be;
(b) by adding to the record a statement specifying in what respect the information
is incomplete, incorrect or misleading; or
(c) by deleting information from the record.
(2) A request under subsection (1) shall, so far as is practicable sufficiently identify the
record concerned and specify the amendment requested.
(3) The public authority concerned shall, within ten (10) working days after receipt of
a request, acknowledge receipt of the request and shall, within thirty (30) days after receipt
of the request, decide whether to grant or refuse the request and shall give notice, in writing
of the decision of the authority to the person concerned.
(4) A public authority may extend the original period of thirty (30) days by such further
period, not exceeding thirty (30) days, as the authority considers necessary if, in the opinion
of the head of the authority, compliance with the original period of thirty (30) days is not
reasonably practicable.

(5) Where a period is extended by a public authority under subsection (4), the authority
shall, before the expiration of the original period, give notice in writing to the applicant
concerned of the extension and the reasons for it.
(6) A notice referred to in subsection (3) shall:
(a) if the decision is to refuse the request, specify the reasons for the refusal;
(b) if the decision is to grant the request, specify the manner in which the record
concerned is to be amended; and
(c) indicate the provisions of this Ordinance regarding review or appeal of a
decision under this section.
(7) A public authority shall provide evidence that the record has been amended by
permitting the applicant to view the amended record or by supplying the applicant with a
copy of the amended record.
(8) No fee is payable in respect of a request under this section.

### 40.

Repeated requests.
(1) A public authority dealing with a request under this Part for access to documents,
or an application under section 39 for the correction of personal information, may refuse to
grant access to documents, or to correct personal information, if the public authority is
satisfied that:
(a) the request or application is made by, or on behalf of, a person who, on at least
one previous occasion, has made a request or application to the public authority,
or to a predecessor of the public authority, for access to the same documents
or for the same correction;
(b) the request or application was refused and the High Court, on reviewing the
decision to refuse the request or application, confirmed the decision; and
(c) there are no reasonable grounds for making the request or application again.
(2) A public authority shall give notice of a refusal under subsection (1) and shall
inform the applicant of the reason for the refusal, the right to apply to the Supreme Court for
judicial review of the decision and the time within which the application for review is required
to be made.

### 41.

Fees in respect of access requests.
(1) No fee is payable simply for the making of a request under this Ordinance for access
to an official document.
(2) Where access to an official document is to be given in the form of printed copies, or
copies in some other form the applicant shall pay the prescribed fee.
(3) The amount of any fee payable by an applicant for the provision of access to
information under this Ordinance shall be calculated by the public authority concerned in
accordance with regulations made under section 73 and shall be commensurate with the cost
incurred in making the information available to the applicant.

(4) Access to information under this Ordinance shall not be provided until any fee in
respect of the provision of access has been paid.
(5) Notwithstanding subsection (2), where a public authority fails to comply with
section 29, any access to official documents to which the applicant is entitled pursuant to his
request shall be provided free of charge.

## PART V-EXEMPT RECORDS

### 42.

Documents affecting health or safety of individual.
(1) Subject to subsection (2), a record is exempt from disclosure if its disclosure would,
or would be likely to, endanger the physical or mental health or the safety of an individual.
(2) A document as described in subsection (1) shall nevertheless be disclosed if
disclosure of it is in the public interest.

### 43.

Documents affecting personal privacy or containing personal information.
(1) A document is an exempt document if its disclosure under this Ordinance would
involve the unreasonable disclosure of personal information of any individual (including a
deceased individual).
(2) Subsection (1) does not apply if:
(a) subject to subsection (3), the requested information concerns the applicant;
(b) the individual to whom the information relates consents in writing to its
disclosure;
(c) the information in the record is of the same kind as information that:
(i) is contained in the record in respect of individuals generally, or in respect
of a class of individuals that is of significant size; and
(ii) is already available to the general public;
(d) the information was given to the public authority concerned by the individual
to whom it relates and that individual was informed on behalf of the authority,
before the information was given, that the information belonged to a class of
information that would or might be made available to the general public; or
(e) disclosure of the information is necessary in order to avoid a serious and
imminent danger to the life or health of an individual.
(3) Where a request by a person other than a person to whom the information requested
refers is made to a public authority for access to a document containing personal information
of any individual (including a deceased individual) and the public authority decides to grant
access to the document, the public authority shall, if practicable, notify the individual who is
the subject of that information (or in the case of a deceased individual, that individual’s nextof-kin) of the decision and of the right to apply to the High Court for judicial review of the
decision and the time within which the application for review is required to be made.
(4) Where the record requested contains medical or psychiatric information or is a
record kept for the purposes of social work in relation to the applicant and the information

sought is likely to be prejudicial to the physical or mental health of the applicant, a public
authority may refuse to grant the request to the applicant but may if requested to do so by
the applicant, provide access to a health professional who has expertise in relation to the
subject matter of the record.
(5) A public authority may grant a request for a record containing personal information
in relation to an individual where the applicant concerned is:
(a) the parent or guardian of the individual, if the individual is under the age of
eighteen; or
(b) the executor or administrator of the estate of the individual.
(6) A record that contains personal information relating to an individual shall
nevertheless be disclosed if disclosure of it is in the public interest or would benefit the
individual.
(7) Subject to subsection (2), “personal information” is as defined in Section 2 of this
Ordinance but does not include:
(a) information about an individual, except where the individual is a police officer
of the Saint Christopher and Nevis Police Force who is or was engaged in a
surveillance function during the course of his employment; who is or was an
officer or employee of a public authority that relates to the position or functions
of the individual;
(b) information about an individual who is or was performing services under
contract for a public authority that relates to the services performed, including
the terms of the contract and the name of the individual; or
(c) information relating to any discretionary benefit of a financial nature, including
the granting of a licence or permit, conferred on an individual by a public
authority, including the name of the individual and the exact nature of the
benefit.

### 44.

Commercial information.
(1) Subject to subsections (2) and (3), a document that consists of the following
information is exempt from disclosure:
(a) trade secrets of any person;
(b) information, the commercial value of which would be, or could reasonably be
expected to be, destroyed or diminished by disclosure;
(c) information, the disclosure of which would have, or could reasonably be
expected to have an adverse effect on the commercial interests of any person
to whom the information relates; or
(d) information, the disclosure of which would prejudice, or could reasonably be
expected to prejudice, the conduct or outcome of contractual or other
negotiations of any person to whom the information relates.

(2) In deciding whether disclosure of information would expose a commercial entity to
disadvantage, a public authority may take account of any of the following considerations:
(a) whether the information is generally available to competitors of the commercial
entity;
(b) whether the information could be disclosed without causing substantial harm
to the competitive position of the commercial entity; and
(c) whether there are any considerations in the public interest in favour of
disclosure which outweigh considerations of competitive disadvantage to the
commercial entity;
(3) Prior to making a determination under subsection (2) as to whether the disclosure of
information would expose a commercial entity to disadvantage, a public authority shall
notify the commercial entity which has supplied the relevant documents that the public
authority has received a request for access to the information and shall:
(a) seek the commercial entity’s views as to whether disclosure should occur; and
(b)

where the public authority after consultation has decided to disclose the
document, notify the commercial entity of this decision and of the right to
apply to the High Court for judicial review and the time within which the
application for review is required to be made.

(4) Subsection (1) does not apply if:
(a) the information concerned relates to the applicant;
(b) the person to whom the information relates consents in writing to its disclosure;
or
(c) the information was given to the public authority concerned by the person to
whom it relates and the person was informed on behalf of the authority, before
the information was given, that the information belonged to a class of
information that would or might be made available to the general public.
(5) A record containing commercial information shall nevertheless be disclosed if
disclosure of it is in the public interest.

### 45.

General information received in confidence.
(1) Subject to subsection (2), a record that consists of the following information is
exempt from disclosure:
(a) information:
(i) that is given to a public authority by a third party (other than another public
authority) in confidence on the understanding that it would be treated as
confidential; and
(ii) the disclosure of which would be likely to prevent the authority from receiving
further similar information required by the authority to properly fulfil its
functions; or

(iii) information, the disclosure of which would constitute a breach of a duty of
confidence provided for by a provision of law.
(2) A record containing confidential information shall be disclosed if disclosure of it is
in the public interest.

### 46.

Information received in confidence related to international tax agreements.
(1) Subject to subsection (2), a record is an exempt record if it is:
(a) a record of a request that has been submitted to the Minister of Finance for his
consideration, being a record that was brought into existence in connection
with an international tax agreement;
(b) an official record of any deliberation or decision by the Minister of Finance in
connection with an international tax agreement;
(c) a record that is a draft or copy of, or an extract from, a record referred to in
paragraph (a) or (b); or
(d) a record, the disclosure of which would involve, or could reasonably be expected
to involve, the disclosure of any deliberation or decision of the Minister of
Finance, other than a record by which a decision of the Minister of Finance is
officially published.
(2) Subsection (1) does not apply to a record that contains purely statistical, technical
or scientific material unless the disclosure of the record would involve, or could reasonably
be expected to involve, the disclosure of any deliberation or decision of the Minister of
Finance.

### 47.

Cabinet documents.
(1) Subject to subsections (2) and (3), a record is an exempt record if it is:
(a) a document that has been prepared by a Minister of Government or on his
behalf by a public authority for the purpose of submission for consideration
by Cabinet or a document which has been considered by the Cabinet and
which is related to issues that are or have been before Cabinet;
(b) an official record of any deliberation or decision of the Cabinet;
(c) drafting instructions, draft bills, draft statutory instruments or a record of
drafting instructions, draft bills, or draft statutory instruments;
(d) a record that is a draft or copy of, or an extract from, a record referred to in
paragraph (a) or (b); or
(e) a record, the disclosure of which would involve, or could reasonably be
expected to involve, the disclosure of any deliberation or decision of the
Cabinet, other than a record by which a decision of the Cabinet is officially
published.
(2) Subsection (1) does not apply to a record that contains purely statistical, technical
or scientific material unless the disclosure of the record would involve, or could reasonably
be expected to involve, the disclosure of any deliberation or decision of the Cabinet.

(3) In this section, “Cabinet” includes any committee or subcommittee of the Cabinet.

### 48.

Ministerial responsibility.
(1) Subject to subsection (2), a record is exempt from disclosure if it consists of
information, the disclosure of which would undermine, or could reasonably be expected to
undermine, individual ministerial responsibility, including free and frank discussion and
advice between Ministers, or between Ministers and public officers, in the course of their
public duties.
(2) A record as described in subsection (1) shall be disclosed if disclosure of it is in the
public interest.

### 49.

Deliberations of public authorities.
(1) Subject to subsections (2) and (3), a record is exempt from disclosure if it consists
of information, the disclosure of which would undermine, or could reasonably be expected to
undermine, the deliberative process of a public authority, including free and frank discussion
and provision of advice in the course of that process.
(2) Subsection (1) does not apply to information contained in a record that is:
(a) factual or statistical information;
(b) the analysis, interpretation or evaluation of, or any projection based on factual
information;
(c) information resulting from an investigation or analysis of the performance,
efficiency or effectiveness of a public authority in relation to its functions;
(d) information in the nature of a report, study or analysis of a scientific or technical
expert including a report by a valuator, whether or not the valuator is an officer
of the public authority; or
(e) an environmental impact statement or similar record;
(f) a final plan or proposal to change a programme of a public authority, or for the
establishment of a new programme, including a budgetary estimate for the
programme, whether or not the plan or proposal is subject to approval, unless
the plan or proposal is to be submitted to Cabinet or a committee or
subcommittee of Cabinet;
(g) a report of a committee, council or other body which is attached to a public
authority and which has been established for the purpose of undertaking
inquiries and making reports and recommendations to the public authority;
(h) information in the nature of the reasons of a public authority for making a
particular decision whether or not the reasons are contained in an internal
memorandum of the public authority or in a letter from an officer or employee of
the public authority.
(3) A record containing deliberations of public authorities shall be disclosed if disclosure
of it is in the public interest.

### 50.

Operations of public authorities.
(1) Subject to subsection (2), a record is exempt if its disclosure could reasonably be
expected to:
(a) prejudice the effectiveness of tests, examinations, investigations, inquiries or
audits conducted by or on behalf of the public authority concerned or the
procedures or methods employed for the conduct of those tests, examinations,
investigations, inquiries or audits;
(b) have a significant, adverse effect on the performance by the public authority of
any of its functions relating to management; or
(c) disclose positions taken, or to be taken, or plans, procedures, criteria or
instructions used or followed, or to be used or followed, for the purpose of any
ongoing negotiations by or on behalf of the Nevis Island Administration or a
public authority.
(2) A record relating to the operations of public authorities shall be disclosed if disclosure
of it is in the public interest.

### 51.

Financial and economic interests.
(1) A document is an exempt document if(a) its disclosure, or premature disclosure, could reasonably be expected to have
a serious adverse effect on the financial interests of the Island of Nevis or on
the ability of the Nevis Island Administration to manage the economy including
but not limited to, the premature disclosure of proposed introduction, abolition
or variation of any tax, duty, interest rate, exchange rate or instrument of
economic management;
(b) its disclosure under this Ordinance would be contrary to the financial interests
of the public authority by giving an unreasonable advantage to any person in
relation to a contract which that person is seeking to enter into with the public
authority.
(2) A record as described in subsection (1) shall be disclosed if disclosure of it is in the
public interest.

### 52.

National security and defence.
A record is exempt from disclosure if disclosure of it would prejudice, or could reasonably
be expected to prejudice, the security or defence of the Federation of Saint Christopher and
Nevis or which would be likely to prejudice the lawful activities of the security or intelligence
services.

### 53.

International relations documents.
(1) A document is an exempt document if its disclosure(a) would prejudice relations between the Government of the Saint Christopher
and Nevis and the government of any other State;

(b) would prejudice relations between the Government of the Saint Christopher
and Nevis and an international organisation of States or a body thereof;
(c) would divulge any information or matter communicated in confidence by or on
behalf of the government of another State to the Government of
Saint Christopher and Nevis or to a person receiving a communication on
behalf of the government of that State; or
(d) would divulge any information communicated in confidence by or on behalf of
an international organisation of States or a body thereof to the Government of
the Saint Christopher and Nevis or to a person receiving a communication on
behalf of that international organisation or body.
(2) A record as described in subsection (1) shall be disclosed if disclosure of it is in the
public interest.

### 54.

Documents affecting enforcement and administration of the law.
(1) Subject to subsection (2), a record is exempt if its disclosure would, or could
reasonably be expected to:
(a) prejudice the prevention, detection or investigation of a breach or possible
breach of the law;
(b) prejudice the enforcement of, compliance with, or administration of, any law;
(c) prejudice the fair trial of a person or the impartial adjudication of a particular
case;
(d) disclose, or enable a person to ascertain, the existence or identity of a
confidential source of information in relation to the enforcement or
administration of the law;
(e) disclose methods or procedures for preventing, detecting, investigating, or
dealing with matters arising out of breaches or evasions of the law, the disclosure
of which would prejudice, or could reasonably be expected to prejudice, the
effectiveness of those methods or procedures;
(f) endanger the lives or physical safety of persons engaged in or in connection
with law enforcement or persons who have provided confidential information
in relation to the enforcement or administration of the law.
(g) prejudice the security of any building, structure, vehicle, ship, boat or aircraft;
or
(h) facilitate the commission of an offence.
(2) This section does not apply to any document that consists of:
(a) information revealing that the scope of a law enforcement investigation is not
authorised by law, or has exceeded the limits imposed by law;
(b) information revealing the use of illegal methods or procedures for preventing,
detecting, investigating, or dealing with matters arising out of breaches or
evasions of the law;

(c) a general outline of the structure of a program adopted by an agency for
dealing with a contravention or possible contravention of the law;
(d) a report on the degree of success achieved in a program adopted by a public
authority for dealing with any breaches of, or enforcing or administering the
law;
(e) a report prepared in the course of a routine law enforcement inspection or
investigation by a public authority whose functions include that of enforcing
and regulating compliance with a particular law other than the criminal law;
(f) a report on a law enforcement investigation the substance of which has already
been disclosed to the person or body that is the subject of the investigation.
(3) A record as described in subsection (1) shall be disclosed if disclosure of it is in the
public interest.

### 55.

Documents affecting legal proceedings or subject to legal professional privilege.
(1) A document is an exempt document if its disclosure under this Ordinance would be
reasonably likely to have a substantial adverse effect on the interests of the Government or
of a prescribed authority in relation to, pending or likely legal proceedings.
(2) Subject to subsection (3), a record is exempt if it is of such a nature that it would be
exempt from production in legal proceedings on the ground of legal professional privilege.
(3) Subject to subsection (4), a record shall be disclosed if disclosure of it is in the
public interest.
(4) For the avoidance of doubt a record held by the Attorney General, the Director of
Public Prosecutions and the Legal Advisor to the Nevis Island Administration that is the
subject of legal professional privilege, shall be an exempt record and shall not be subject to
public disclosure of any kind.

### 56.

Documents the disclosure of which would be contempt of court and breach of
parliamentary privilege.
A record is exempt if its disclosure would, or could reasonably be expected to be:
(a) in contempt of court or contrary to an order made by a court, tribunal or other
judicial body; or
(b) an infringement of parliamentary privilege.

### 57.

Disclosure prohibited by other legislation.
(1) A record is exempt if its disclosure is prohibited by any statutory provision, other
than this Ordinance.
(2) Where a statutory provision made after the coming into operation of this section
prohibits the disclosure of a record, the provision shall not have effect unless it provides
specifically that it is to have effect notwithstanding this Ordinance.

### 58.

Disclosure of exempt document in the public interest.
(1) Notwithstanding any law to the contrary a public authority shall give access to an
exempt document where there is reasonable evidence that significant:
(a) abuse of authority or neglect in the performance of official duty; or
(b) injustice to an individual; or
(c) danger to the health or safety of an individual or of the public; or
(d) unauthorised use of public funds,
has or is likely to have occurred or in the circumstances giving access to the document is
justified in the public interest having regard to any benefit and to any damage that may result
from disclosure.
(2) For the purposes of this Part, the test of whether disclosure by a public authority of
information is in the public interest is whether the public interest would be better served by
disclosure than by non-disclosure.

### 59.

Certification of an exempt document.
For the purposes of this Ordinance, a certificate signed by the Secretary to the Cabinet
or a person performing the duties of the Secretary, certifying that a document is one of a kind
referred to in this Part establishes conclusively that it is an exempt document.

### 60.

Non-disclosure of existence of a record.
(1) A public authority may refuse to disclose whether a record exists if the record itself,
if it exists or were to exist, is or would be an exempt record.
(2) The existence or non-existence of a record shall be disclosed if disclosure of it is in
the public interest.

### 61.

Notice to third parties.
(1) If a public authority intends to disclose a record that the head of the public authority
has reason to believe might contain information of a type referred to in section 43, 44 or 45 the
head of the public authority shall give notice to any third party who submitted the information
to the authority, or to whom the information relates and a copy of the said notice to the
applicant.
(2) A notice under subsection (1) to a third party shall:
(a) state that the authority intends to disclose a record that might contain information
of a type referred to in section 43,44 or 45, that was given to the authority by
the third party or that relates to the third party, as the case may be;
(b) describe the contents of the record; and
(c) state that, within fourteen days after the notice is given, the third party may
consent in writing to the disclosure of the record or make written representations
to the authority with regard to the intended disclosure.

(3) The public authority shall consider any representations made by a third party
pursuant to a notice under this section before making a decision whether to grant or refuse
to grant a request for access to a record referred to in this section.

### 62.

Expiry of certain exemptions.
(1) Subject to subsection (2), a record shall not be exempt under this Part after the
expiry of 30 years from the date when the record was created.
(2) Subsection (1) does not apply to a record that is exempt from disclosure under
section 43 or 57.
PARTVI- INTERNAL REVIEW

### 63.

Internal review by authority.
An applicant or a third party may apply in writing to a public authority for a review by
the authority of any decision made by the authority with respect to a request made under

## Part IV or of any failure by the authority to take any action that it is required to take under this

Ordinance in respect of such request, including:
(a) a decision to grant or refuse to grant access to a record;
(b) a decision as to the manner in which access to a record requested is to be
provided;
(c) a decision to transfer a request to another authority, in whole or in part;
(d) a decision to extend the time limit for compliance with a request;
(e) a decision as to the fee charged for access to a record;
(f) a decision to refuse a request to amend a record in respect of personal
information;
(g) a decision to refuse to disclose the existence of a record; and
(h) a failure to do anything relating to a request within the time required by this
Ordinance.

### 64.

Time limit to apply for internal review.
(1) In the case of a decision by a public authority referred to in section 63, an application
for an internal review must be made within thirty (30) days after the date when the applicant
or third party, as the case may be, is notified of the decision.
(2) In the case of failure by a public authority to take any action with regard to a request
made under Part IV within the time limits required by this Ordinance, an application for
internal review must be made within thirty (30) days after the date when the authority was
required to take such action.
(3) A public authority may in its discretion extend the period for making an application
for internal review.

### 65.

Conduct of review.
(1) An internal review of a decision by a public authority shall be conducted by the
head of the public authority concerned unless the decision being reviewed was made by the
head of a public authority.
(2) The head of the public authority shall within twenty-one (21) days after receiving
an application for an internal review with respect to a request:
(a) complete the review and make a decision with regard to the review; and
(b) notify the applicant and any third party concerned of:
i.

the decision and the reasons for the decision; and

ii.

the right of the applicant or third party, as the case may be, to apply to the
Commissioner for a review of the decision under Part VII.

### 66.

Referral to Commissioner.
(1) Where an application to a public authority for internal review is for review of a
decision made by the head of the public authority, the public authority shall refer the
application to the Commissioner.
(2) Where an application for an internal review is referred to the Commissioner, it shall
be treated as an application for a review under Part VII and dealt with accordingly.
(3) The public authority concerned shall, within five (5) working days after receiving
an application under this section, notify the person who made the application that the
application has been referred to the Commissioner under this section and is to be treated as
an application under Part VII.

### 67.

Burden of proof.
In proceedings under this Part, the Ministry or prescribed authority to which or to whom
the request was made has the onus of establishing that a decision given in respect of the
request was justified.

## PART VII- REVIEW BY COMMISSIONER OF INFORMATION

### 68.

Application for review.
(1) Subject to subsection (2), an applicant or a third party may apply in writing to the
Commissioner for a review, as the case may be, of:
(a) any decision made by the head of a public authority under section 63, within
thirty (30) days after being notified of that decision; or
(b) any failure by the head of a public authority to make a decision under section
63, within thirty (30) days after the date when the decision was required to be
made.
(2) The Commissioner may in his discretion extend the period for a person to apply for
a review under this Part.

### 69.

Alternative dispute resolution.
(1) The Commissioner may at any time attempt to have the matter that is the subject of
an application for review resolved by alternative dispute resolution including negotiation,
conciliation, mediation or otherwise.
(2) The Commissioner may authorize any person appointed or engaged under
section 12 to act as a mediator or conciliator as the case may be or may appoint any person
appointed or engaged under section 12 to take part in the proceedings in any capacity he
deems necessary and shall decide the procedure governing the proceedings.
(3) Participation in an alternative form of dispute resolution shall be voluntary and any
party to it may withdraw at any time.
(4) Anything said or admitted during the attempt at an alternative form of dispute
resolution and any document prepared pursuant thereto shall not be admissible in evidence
against any person in any subsequent proceedings concerning a matter that is the subject of
the negotiation, conciliation or mediation as the case may be and no evidence in respect of
any such exercise may be given against any person.

### 70.

Review.
(1) If the Commissioner decides not to attempt to resolve the matter under section 69 or
if any such attempt is unsuccessful, the Commissioner shall commence a review of the
matter.
(2) Subject to this Ordinance, the Commissioner may determine the procedure to be
followed in the conduct of a review under this Part.
(3) Every review shall be conducted in private.
(4) The Commissioner shall give a reasonable opportunity for the applicant, the
public authority and any third party concerned to make representations.
(5) Unless permitted by the Commissioner, no person has a right to be present during
representations made to the Commissioner by another person, or the right to have access to,
or to comment on, representations made by another person.
(6) The Commissioner shall, as soon as practicable:
(a) complete the review and make a decision with regard to the review; and
(b) notify the applicant, the public authority and any third party concerned of the
Commissioner’s decision and the reasons for that decision.

### 71.

Decision by Commissioner.
(1) The Commissioner may make a decision to:
(a) affirm, vary or reverse the decision of a public authority that is the subject of
review by the Commissioner; or
(b) make such other order, in accordance with this Ordinance, as the Commissioner
deems appropriate.

(2) A decision of the Commissioner shall, where appropriate, specify the period within
which effect shall be given to the decision.
(3) Subject to section 72, a decision of the Commissioner is binding on all persons
affected by it and, upon the decision being filed with the Registrar of the Supreme Court, it
shall have the effect of an order of the Supreme Court and shall be enforceable in the same
manner as an order of the court.

### 72.

Judicial review.
(1) Any person (including any public authority) aggrieved by a decision of the
Commissioner under this Ordinance may apply to the Supreme Court for a review of the
decision and the Court may confirm, vary, remit or set aside the decision.
(2) An application under subsection (1) for judicial review shall be heard and determined
by a judge in chambers unless the Court, with the consent of the parties, directs otherwise.
(3) In this section, “decision of a public authority” includes the failure of a public
authority to comply with section 29 or 30 (1).

## PART VIII- MISCELLANEOUS

### 73.

Regulations.
(1) The Minister may make regulations not inconsistent with this Ordinance prescribing
all matters that by this Ordinance are required or permitted to be prescribed, or are necessary
or convenient to be prescribed for carrying out or giving effect to this Ordinance, and in
particular, making provision for or in relation to:
(a) prescribing fees payable for the provision of access to records under this
Ordinance, including the manner of calculating fees, circumstances in which
no fee is payable, the maximum fee that may be charged and requiring deposits
on account of such charges or fees;
(b) amending the Schedule by the addition, modification or deletion of the name of
any public authority or head of a public authority;
(c) the officers who may give decisions on behalf of a Ministry or prescribed
authority;
(d) providing for the management and maintenance of records held by public
authorities; and
(e) generally for carrying out the purposes and provisions of this Ordinance.
(2) Regulations made under this Ordinance may provide for the imposition of a fine not
exceeding ten thousand dollars or imprisonment for a period not exceeding six months or
both for the contravention of any provision of the regulations.

### 74.

Ordinance binds the Crown.
This Ordinance binds the Crown, the Nevis Island Administration and all authorised
authorities.

### 75.

Codes of practice.
(1) The Minister shall, following consultation with the Commissioner, establish codes
of practice for public authorities regarding the administration of this Ordinance.
(2) The Minister shall, in consultation with the Commissioner and the Director of the
Department of Information, establish codes of practice for public authorities regarding the
maintenance and management of records of public authorities in a manner that facilitates
ready access to the records.

### 76.

Training and organisation.
The Minister shall ensure that appropriate measures are taken by public authorities with
regard to training of staff, organisational arrangements and any other matters that the Minister
considers appropriate, for the purposes of facilitating compliance with this Ordinance.

### 77.

Information officer.
(1) Every public authority shall designate one of its officers to be the person to whom
requests under the Ordinance may be directed and ensure that members of the public have
easy access to the information officer, including his or her name, function and contact
details.
(2) The information officer shall, in addition to any obligations specifically provided
for in other provisions of this Ordinance, have the following responsibilities:
(a) to serve as a central contact within the public authority for receiving requests
for information, for assisting persons seeking to obtain information and for
receiving complaints regarding the performance of the public authority relating
to the provision of information pursuant to this Ordinance;
(b) to promote within the public authority the best possible practices in relation to
record maintenance, archiving and disposal.
(3) The Commissioner of Information shall be responsible for the training of information
officers in each year to better enable them to discharge their duties under this Ordinance.

### 78.

Protection against liability.
(1) No proceedings, civil or criminal, shall be brought against any public authority, its
employees or agents in respect of the disclosure or non-disclosure by any of them of any
record under this Ordinance, or any action taken or omitted to be taken under this Ordinance,
unless it is shown that the authority, employee or agent acted in bad faith.
(2) Where access has been given to a document and:
(a) the access was required by this Ordinance to be given; or
(b) the access was authorised by a Minister or by an officer having authority to
make decisions in respect of requests, in the bona fide belief that the access
was required by this Ordinance to be given, neither the person authorising the
access nor any person concerned in the giving of the access is guilty of a
criminal offence by reason only of the authorising or giving of the access.

(3) The giving of access to a document, including an exempt document, in furtherance
of a request shall not be taken for the purposes of the law relating to defamation, breach of
confidence or copyright, to constitute an authorisation or approval of the publication of the
document or its contents by the person to whom access is given.

### 79.

Offence to alter or destroy record.
(1) Any person who, subject to subsection (2), alters, erases, destroys or conceals any
record with the intention of preventing its disclosure under this Ordinance commits an
offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine of $10,000 or to imprisonment for six
months or to both such fine and imprisonment.
(2) A person who knowingly destroys or damages a record or document which is
required to be maintained and preserved under subsection (1) while a request for access to
the record or document is pending commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction
to a fine of ten thousand dollars and imprisonment for two years.

### 80.

Commencement.
(1) This Ordinance comes into operation on a day to be appointed by the Minister by
notice published in the Gazette.
(2) The Minister may appoint different days for different provisions of this Ordinance.
