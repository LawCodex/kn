### 1.

CITATION AND COMMENCEMENT.
This Order may be cited as the Nevis Air and Sea Ports Authority (Amendment) (Tariff
Book) Regulations, 2018, and shall be deemed to have come into force on the 1st day of
March, 2018.

### 2.

AMENDMENT OF INTERPRETATION.
Interpretation to the Nevis Air and Sea Ports Authority Act (Tariff Book) Regulations,
2002 is amended by adding the following:
“Shipper/Consignor” means an individual or firm that sends freight. Afreight originator.
“Consignee” means an individual or firm to who freight is shipped. A freight receiver.
“Freight Bill-of-Lading (Freight Bill, BL or BOL)” means a document providing or binding
contract between a shipper and a carrier for the transportation of freight, specifying
the obligations of both parties. Serves as a receipt of freight by the carrier for the
shipper. Usually designates the consignee, and the FOB point.
“Berth” means a place in which a vessel is moored or secured: place alongside a quay
where a ship loads or discharges cargo.
“Berth Dues (or quay dues or dockage)” means charges for the use of a berth. Typically
assessed based on the duration of a vessel’s stay and length overall (LOA).
“Bill of Lading” means a document that establishes the terms of contract between a
shipper and a transportation company. It serves as a document of title, a contract
of carriage and a receipt for goods.
“Breakbulk” means Loose, non-containerised cargo stowed directly into ship’s hold.
“Broker” means a person who arranges for transportation of loads for a percentage of
the revenue from the load.
“Harbour Dues (or port dues)” means charges by a port authority to a vessel for each
harbour entry, usually on a per gross tonnage basis, to cover the costs of basic
port infrastructure such and marine facilities such as buoys, beacons, and vessel
traffic management system.

“Overlanded Cargo” means cargo which has been discharged or a port for which it was
not intended according to the manifest of the ship.
“Navigational Aid (also known as aid to navigation, ATON, or navaid)” means any sort
of marker which aids the traveler in navigation: the term is most commonly used to
refer to nautical or aviation travel, common types of such aids include lighthouses,
buoys, fog signals and day beacons.
“Moorings” means the means by which a ship, boat or aircraft is secured E.g. anchor,
floating buoy.
“Piloting” a pilot means a mariner who manoeuvres ships through dangerous or
congested waters, such as harbours or river mouths, and completes the berthing/
unberthing operation of the ships by controlling the ship’s manoeuvrability directly
and the tugs and shore linesmen through a radio. Pilots are expert ship handlers
who possess detailed knowledge of local waterways.

### 3.

AMENDMENT OF SCHEDULE II.
The Second Schedule to the Nevis Air and Sea Ports Authority Act (Tariff Book)
Regulations, 2002 is amended by deleting “items 03 and 07” and replacing it with the new
“items 03 and 07.

### 4.

AMENDMENT OF SCHEDULE III.
The Third Schedule to the Nevis Air and Sea Ports Authority Act (Tariff Book)
Regulations, 2002 is amended by adding a new “15” and by deleting “items 23, 25 and 27”
and replacing it with the new “items 23, 25 and 27.”

### 5.

AMENDMENT OF SCHEDULE IV.
The Fourth Schedule to the Nevis Air and Sea Ports Authority Act (Tariff Book)
Regulations, 2002 is amended by deleting “items 34, 35, 37, 39, 46, 48 and 51” and replacing
it with the new “items 34, 35, 37, 39, 46, 48 and 51.”

### 6.

AMENDMENT OF SCHEDULE V.
The Fifth Schedule to the Nevis Air and Sea Ports Authority Act (Tariff Book)
Regulations, 2002 is amended by deleting “item 53” and replacing it with the new “item 53.”

### 7.

AMENDMENT OF SCHEDULE VI.
The Sixth Schedule to the Nevis Air and Sea Ports Authority Act (Tariff Book)
Regulations, 2002 is amended by deleting “items 61, 65, 71, 73, 74, 75, 76 and 77” and
replacing it with the new items 61, 65, 71, 73, 74, 75, 76 and 77. ”
